**Type:** Visual discussion activity  
**Time:** 10–15 minutes  
**Group Size:** 3+  
**Materials:**
- A small smooth stone or pebble
- Paper
- Pencils or markers

### Purpose
Help children identify fears and problems while shifting their attention toward God's greatness and faithfulness.

### Setup
Give each child a piece of paper.
Ask them to draw or write something that sometimes feels "big" to them.

Younger children can draw a picture.
Older children can write a word or short description.

Examples:
- Fear
- School
- Friendship problems
- Being left out
- Making a difficult choice
- Something they are worried about

### Activity
Hold up the small stone.
Say:
*"David's weapon looked small compared with Goliath. But David's confidence wasn't really in the stone."*

Ask:
*"Where was David's confidence?"*
**In God.**

Have children write or draw their "giant problem."
Then have them write underneath:
**GOD IS GREATER.**

### Discussion
Ask:
*"Does writing this mean the problem isn't real?"*
No.

Explain:
*"David knew Goliath was real and dangerous. Trusting God doesn't mean pretending our problems aren't real. It means remembering that our problems are not bigger than God."*

<AgeGroup level="upper-elementary">
### Older Child Variation
Have older children write:

- **My problem:** _______
- **What fear tells me:** _______
- **What I know about God:** _______
- **My next right step:** _______

This helps move the activity from vague encouragement toward practical faith.
</AgeGroup>

### Take-Home Connection
Let children take their paper home.
Encourage them to look at it during the week whenever fear returns.
