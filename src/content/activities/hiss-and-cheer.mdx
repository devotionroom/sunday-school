### Purpose

To introduce children to the Jewish tradition of Purim through an active, playful game that illustrates the great reversal at the heart of the Esther story — the villain's plan came to nothing, and the heroes were honoured.

### Background for the Teacher

At Purim, Jewish people celebrate by reading the book of Esther aloud in the synagogue. Every time Haman's name is mentioned, the congregation makes loud noise — stomping, rattling, and booing — to "drown out" his name. This ancient, joyful tradition is based on the idea that Haman's name should be blotted out. Every time Mordecai or Esther's name is mentioned, they cheer!

### Setup

No special setup required. If you have cups and rice available, help the children quickly make noisemakers before the lesson begins. Otherwise, hands clapping and feet stomping work perfectly.

### How to Play

1. **Explain the tradition:** Tell the children that at Purim, Jewish people celebrate by reading the book of Esther. Every time Haman's name is mentioned, they make noise to "drown out" his name. Every time Mordecai or Esther's name is mentioned, they cheer!

2. **Teach the signals:**
   - 🚨 **"HAMAN"** → shake noisemakers, stomp feet, or boo loudly.
   - 🎉 **"MORDECAI" or "ESTHER"** → cheer and clap!

3. **Play the game:** The teacher reads short sentences or events from the Esther story. Children respond to each name as it comes up.

**Example sentences to read aloud:**
- *"King Xerxes chose ESTHER to be his queen."* → Cheer!
- *"HAMAN demanded that everyone bow down to him."* → Boo!
- *"MORDECAI refused to bow to HAMAN."* → Cheer for Mordecai, boo for Haman!
- *"HAMAN tricked the king into signing a terrible law."* → Boo!
- *"ESTHER fasted and prayed for three days."* → Cheer!
- *"ESTHER went bravely to see the king."* → Cheer!
- *"HAMAN's evil plan was defeated!"* → Boo (turning into laughter)!
- *"MORDECAI was honoured by the king!"* → Cheer!
- *"ESTHER and MORDECAI gave gifts to the poor to celebrate!"* → Cheer!

### Discussion

After the game, ask:

- *"Why do you think Jewish people drown out Haman's name? What does that tell us about evil plans?"*
- *"How do you think Mordecai and Esther felt when God turned everything around?"*
- *"This celebration is called Purim — it has been celebrated for thousands of years! What does that tell us about how much it means to God's people?"*

<AgeGroup level="preschool">
### For Younger Children

Keep it simple: **stomp for the bad guy, clap for the good guys.** The physical energy of stomping and cheering helps very young children feel the story in their bodies — the bad man lost, the good people won, and everyone celebrated!
</AgeGroup>

<AgeGroup level="upper-elementary">
### For Older Children

After the game, explain that this practice of making noise to "blot out" Haman's name has been part of Jewish life for over 2,000 years. It comes from Deuteronomy 25:19 ("blot out the memory of Amalek") — Haman was descended from the Amalekites. Ask:

- *"Why do you think this tradition lasted thousands of years? What does it do for people who have faced real persecution?"*
- *"The book of Esther never mentions God's name. But we can see God's fingerprints everywhere. Where do you see them?"*
- *"Purim is called a celebration of 'reversal.' Can you think of an even greater reversal in the New Testament?"* (The cross: what looked like defeat was actually the greatest victory.)
</AgeGroup>
