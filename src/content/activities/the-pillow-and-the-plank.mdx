### Purpose
To demonstrate visually that the stability of a structure does not depend on the beauty or size of the building, but entirely on the firmness of what lies underneath it.

### Setup
1. Place the soft pillow on one side of a low table or floor.
2. Place the flat wooden board or heavy hardcover book next to it.
3. Have two identical stacks of 10 building blocks or plastic cups ready.

### Instructions
1. Invite two volunteers to the front.
2. Hand one child the blocks for the pillow ("Building on Sand/Fluff") and the other the blocks for the board ("Building on Solid Rock").
3. Challenge both to build the tallest, neatest tower they can in 60 seconds.
4. As soon as the towers are up, gently tap the table or blow air toward both structures with a fan or hairdryer.
5. Watch the tower on the pillow wobble and tumble immediately, while the tower on the board stands upright and solid!
6. Explain: *"Both builders had the exact same blocks! The difference wasn't the blocks—it was what they built upon. Jesus said His words are like the solid board."*

### Age Adaptations
<AgeGroup level="preschool">
Allow each preschooler to step onto a firm wooden tile or concrete step ("Strong Rock!"), then step onto a squishy cushion ("Wobbly Sand!"). Ask: *"Where is it easiest to balance without falling?"*
</AgeGroup>

<AgeGroup level="upper-elementary">
Ask older kids to list common cultural "pillows" that people build on (popularity, wealth, sports achievements, pleasing friends). Discuss why those things wobble and collapse when real trials or rejections occur.
</AgeGroup>
