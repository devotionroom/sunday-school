**Type:** Visual Object Lesson  
**Time:** 10 minutes  
**Group Size:** Any  
**Materials:** 
- A large, colorful picture (a landscape, a famous painting, or a poster).
- Cut the picture into several jigsaw pieces before class. Take one piece that has dark or confusing colors on it (like a shadow or a muddy spot) and set it aside.

### Purpose
To visually demonstrate that while one specific event in our lives might look dark, confusing, or ugly, God is using it to build a beautiful "big picture."

### Setup
Keep the pieces of the puzzle hidden. Hold only the single dark/confusing piece in your hand.

### Exact Instructions
1. Hold up the single, confusing puzzle piece so all the children can see it.
2. Ask the children: *"What is this a picture of? Is it pretty? Does it make any sense?"*
3. Allow them to guess. Acknowledge that on its own, the piece looks like a mistake, a dark smudge, or just a mess.
4. Say: *"Sometimes, things happen in our lives that feel just like this piece. They are confusing, sad, or unfair. If we only look at this one piece, we might think the whole picture is ruined."*
5. Bring out the rest of the puzzle pieces. Invite a few children to help you quickly assemble them on a table or the floor.
6. Finally, place the dark/confusing piece into its exact spot.
7. Say: *"Now look at the big picture! Does this dark piece make sense now? Yes. The picture actually needed this shadow/spot to make the whole image complete."*

### Discussion
Ask:
*"When bad things happen to us, do we get to see the 'big picture' right away?"* (No).
*"Who is the only one who sees the big picture?"* (God).

### Connection Back to the Bible Story
Remind the children that Joseph spent years looking at dark, ugly puzzle pieces—being thrown in a pit, sold as a slave, and locked in a dungeon. It made no sense at the time. But God was putting together a massive, beautiful picture that would eventually save thousands of lives from starvation.
