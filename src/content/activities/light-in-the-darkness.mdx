## Overview

A simple, powerful object lesson that opens the Hanukkah lesson. Children experience a moment of genuine darkness, then see how even a single small light changes everything — illustrating both the Temple rededication and Jesus' declaration that He is the Light of the World.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Safety:** Use ONLY a flashlight or battery-operated candle. No open flames, no matches, no real candles. If covering windows, ensure children remain seated and calm during the dark segment.

## Setup

Before the lesson begins:
- Identify how dark you can make the room (turn off lights, close blinds, cover windows with dark paper if possible).
- Have your flashlight or battery-operated candle ready but switched off.
- Keep children seated so no one trips or bumps during the dark segment.

## Activity Instructions

### Step 1 — Experience the Darkness (about 30 seconds)

Turn off the lights. If the room is not very dark, also cover the windows or close curtains.

Say:
> *"I want us to experience something together. For just a moment, I need everyone to sit still and be quiet. Ready?"*

Allow 30 seconds in the darkness. Then ask (while still dark):
- *"How does the darkness feel?"*
- *"Could you find your way easily if you needed to?"*
- *"If this room were your home — your whole world — and the lights had been off for a very long time, how would that feel?"*

### Step 2 — Turn on the Light

Switch on the flashlight or battery-operated candle.

Ask:
- *"What changed?"*
- *"Did the whole room change, or just part of it?"*
- *"What would happen if this one small light had not been there at all?"*

### Step 3 — Bridge to the Story

Say:
> *"The Temple in Jerusalem was like a room that went dark. A king named Antiochus came and stopped the worship of God. The altar was desecrated. The sacrifices stopped. The light of God's presence seemed to go out. But God preserved His people. The Maccabees fought to restore the Temple and rededicate it to God — and the light came back on.*
>
> *And later, Jesus stood in that very Temple during the Feast of Dedication and said something remarkable: 'I am the light of the world. Whoever follows me will never walk in darkness but will have the light of life.'*
>
> *Today we're going to learn about that story."*

## Discussion Prompts

- What does it feel like when a room is completely dark?
- What does it mean for Jesus to be the "light of the world"?
- If Jesus is a light — what kind of darkness does He push back?

## Age Adaptation

**Preschool:** Keep it short. After turning on the light, simply say: *"The darkness went away! That's what Jesus does — He brings light."* Don't dwell on the historical background; let the object lesson carry the introduction.

**Elementary:** Encourage more thoughtful answers. Ask what it would feel like if the darkness lasted not 30 seconds but months or years. Help them imagine what the Jewish people felt when the Temple was restored.
