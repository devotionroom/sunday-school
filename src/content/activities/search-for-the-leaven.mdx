### Purpose

To introduce children to the Passover story and the Feast of Unleavened Bread through an active, engaging search game. Children hunt for hidden "leaven" around the room, setting up the key truth that Israel left Egypt in such haste that they had no time to let bread rise — a vivid reminder that God's rescue came swiftly and by His mighty power.

### Background for the Teacher

Before Passover begins, Jewish families observe an ancient tradition known as *bedikat chametz* (the search for leaven). In Exodus 12:15–20, God commanded Israel to remove all leaven (yeast) from their homes for seven days, beginning at Passover. 

Why unleavened bread? Exodus 12:34 and 12:39 explain that when Pharaoh finally told Israel to leave, the people were driven out of Egypt in such haste that they could not tarry, nor had they prepared any food except the unleavened dough on their shoulders. They baked unleavened cakes because there was no time for the bread to rise. 

In the New Testament, the Apostle Paul draws on this very imagery in 1 Corinthians 5:7–8: *"Get rid of the old yeast, that you may be a new unleavened batch—as you really are. For Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed."* Leaven in scripture often symbolizes sin that permeates and spreads; removing it points to the purity and new life brought by Christ our Passover Lamb.

### Setup

Before the children arrive, hide 10 small slips of paper labelled **"LEAVEN"** (or small paper cutouts shaped like loaves of bread) in plain sight and under objects around the classroom. Make sure they are safe for children to find (on shelves, under chair seats, behind books, on window sills).

### How to Play

1. **Introduce the Search:** Gather the children and ask: *"Have you ever had to pack your bags and leave somewhere in a super fast hurry?"* Let a few children answer.
2. **Explain the Tradition:** Tell the class: *"Before Passover, Jewish families do something special: they search their entire home for any leaven — that's yeast, the ingredient that makes bread dough puff up and rise. Today, 10 pieces of leaven are hidden in our classroom. When I say 'Go,' your job is to work together and find all 10!"*
3. **The Hunt:** Send the children on the search. Encourage cooperation — older children can help younger ones spot pieces in higher places.
4. **Count and Gather:** As slips are found, place them in a small basket or on the table and count aloud together: *"1, 2, 3... 10! We found all 10!"*
5. **Teach the Lesson:** Hold up the slips and explain:
   - *"On the first Passover night, God told His people to eat flat, unleavened bread with their sandals on, their cloaks tucked in, and their walking sticks in hand."*
   - *"Why? Because they were leaving Egypt in such a hurry! They had no time to wait hours for bread dough to rise. When God said 'Go,' they had to be ready instantly."*
   - *"This leaven search reminds us that God's rescue was swift, complete, and entirely by His power."*

### Discussion & Connection to Christ

After the game, gather the children and ask:
- *"Why didn't the Israelites have time to bake fluffy bread?"* (They had to leave Egypt immediately when Pharaoh let them go.)
- *"What does being ready to leave tell us about trusting God's timing?"* (When God acts, we follow without delay.)
- *"In 1 Corinthians 5:7, Paul says 'Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed.' Just like families cleared out the leaven, Jesus cleanses our hearts from sin so we can live fresh, clean lives for Him."*

<AgeGroup level="preschool">
### For Younger Children

For preschoolers, keep the search cooperative and guided:
- Hide the slips in very easy-to-spot places at eye level.
- Hunt together as one big team rather than competing against one another.
- Focus on the main sensory idea: *"Fluffy bread takes a long time to rise. Flat bread is fast! The Israelites ate flat bread because God rescued them FAST!"*
- Have children practice standing up quickly with pretend walking sticks, ready to walk out of Egypt.
</AgeGroup>

<AgeGroup level="upper-elementary">
### For Older Children

For elementary students, deepen both the historical and biblical meaning:
- Explain the traditional ceremony of *bedikat chametz*: searching by candlelight with a feather and a wooden spoon on the night before Passover, sweeping every crumb into the spoon, wrapping it in cloth, and burning it the next morning.
- Read Exodus 12:34, 39: *"The people took their dough before it was leavened, their kneading bowls being bound up in their cloaks on their shoulders."*
- Read 1 Corinthians 5:6–8 together: Discuss how Paul uses leaven as a picture of sin and malice, while unleavened bread represents sincerity and truth. Connect Christ's sacrifice as our Passover Lamb to the new, sin-cleansed life believers are given.
</AgeGroup>
