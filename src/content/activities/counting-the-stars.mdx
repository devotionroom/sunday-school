**Type:** Visual / Interactive Challenge  
**Time:** 5 minutes  
**Group Size:** Any  
**Materials:** 
- A clear jar filled with something tiny and numerous (e.g., salt, sugar, sand, sprinkles, or hole-punch confetti).
- A dark piece of construction paper.

### Purpose
To visually demonstrate the absolute impossibility of counting the stars, helping children understand the massive scale of the promise God made to Abraham.

### Setup
Place the jar of tiny items and the black paper on a table in front of the children.

### Exact Instructions
1. Call a volunteer to the front. 
2. Say: *"I have a very important job for you. I will give you 30 seconds, and I want you to count exactly how many pieces are in this jar."*
3. Pour a large pile of the salt/sprinkles onto the black paper. 
4. Start the timer. Let the child try to count them by poking them with their finger. They will quickly realize it is impossible.
5. Ask the rest of the children: *"If I gave them all day, could they count them perfectly? What if I gave them a whole year?"* (No, there are too many, they are too small, and they get mixed up).

### Discussion
Ask:
*"Have you ever tried to count the stars in the sky at night? What happens?"* (You lose your place, there are too many, some are too faint).
*"If someone told you they were going to give you as many dollars as there are pieces of salt on this paper, would you believe them?"*

### Connection Back to the Bible Story
Explain:
*"Abraham was an old man, and he didn't have a single child. Not one! But God took him outside, pointed at the night sky, and told him to do exactly what we just tried to do: count the stars. God promised Abraham that his family would be as massive and impossible to count as the stars in the sky. And even though it sounded completely impossible, Abraham chose to believe God."*
