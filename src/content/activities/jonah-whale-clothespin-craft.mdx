### Purpose
To create a fun, interactive toy that helps children retell the story of Jonah being swallowed by the great fish.

### Setup
![Finished Jonah Whale](/sunday-school/crafts/jonah-whale.jpeg)

1. Cut out a profile shape of a whale (or big fish) from the blue paper for each child. The whale should be about 3-4 inches long.
2. Cut each whale in half horizontally, straight through the middle of the mouth! So you have a "top half" of the whale and a "bottom half."
3. Cut out tiny little stick-figure Jonahs from the white paper.

### Instructions
1. **Decorate:** Let the children use markers to draw an eye, fins, and water spouts on the top half of their whale, and a belly on the bottom half. They can also color their tiny Jonah!
2. **The Clothespin:** Have the children take a wooden clothespin and lay it flat on the table.
3. **Assemble the Whale:** 
   - Glue the *top half* of the whale to the *top prong* of the clothespin.
   - Glue the *bottom half* of the whale to the *bottom prong* of the clothespin. 
   - Make sure they line up perfectly when the clothespin is closed!
4. **Add Jonah:** Open the clothespin. Glue the tiny paper Jonah to the *back* of the bottom prong, so that when the clothespin is closed, Jonah is completely hidden.
5. **Play:** Show the children how to pinch the back of the clothespin. The whale's mouth will open wide, revealing Jonah trapped inside!

### Discussion While Crafting
- *"Why did the great fish swallow Jonah?"* (Because Jonah ran away from God and got thrown into the stormy sea).
- *"Was the fish a punishment, or was it a rescue?"* (Both! It stopped Jonah from running, but it also saved him from drowning).
- *"What did Jonah do while he was in the fish's belly?"* (He finally prayed!).
