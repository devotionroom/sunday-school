## Teacher Preparation

**Before class:**
- Read Daniel 6 carefully.
- Pay special attention to verses 3–5, 10, 16–23.
- Prepare the *Stand Firm* activity.
- Write the memory verse somewhere visible.
- Have a Bible available.
- Decide which age adaptation best fits your group.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A note for the teacher**  
> Daniel 6 is sometimes reduced to: *"Daniel prayed, got thrown to the lions, and God saved him."* That leaves out an important part of the lesson. Daniel did **not** know that the lions would be unable to hurt him. He had to choose faithfulness **before** he knew the outcome. That distinction matters. We should not teach children: *"If you obey God, nothing bad will happen."* Instead, teach: **"God is worthy of our faithfulness even when we don't know what will happen."** Daniel's rescue demonstrates God's power and faithfulness. It is not a promise that faithful Christians will never experience suffering or danger.

## Opening: What Would You Do?

Ask the children:
> *"Imagine you are doing something you know is right, but someone tells you that you will get in trouble if you keep doing it. What would you do?"*

Give examples:
- Telling the truth
- Standing up for someone
- Refusing to cheat
- Being kind to someone unpopular
- Praying
- Saying no when friends want you to do something wrong

Ask: *"Is doing the right thing always easy?"*
No.

Say: *"Today we're going to meet someone who had to make exactly that kind of choice."*
His name was Daniel.

## The Story of Daniel

### 1. Daniel Was Faithful

Daniel lived in Babylon and served under King Darius.
Daniel was an excellent worker. The king trusted him. The Bible says Daniel had an excellent spirit in him.

Other officials became jealous. They searched for something wrong that Daniel had done. But they couldn't find anything.
Daniel was trustworthy. He wasn't secretly dishonest. He wasn't abusing his position.

His enemies eventually realized:
> *"We aren't going to find anything against Daniel unless it has something to do with his relationship with God."*

Daniel's faithfulness was part of who he was.

### 2. Daniel's Enemies Made a Trap

Daniel's enemies went to King Darius with a plan.
They convinced the king to make a new law. For thirty days, no one could pray to anyone except the king. Anyone who broke the law would be thrown into a den of lions.

The king signed the law.
Now Daniel had a choice.
He knew the law. He knew the consequences. And he knew what God wanted him to do.

### 3. Daniel Chose to Keep Praying

Daniel went home. He opened his windows toward Jerusalem. He knelt down. And he prayed to God.
Just as he had done before.

Daniel didn't suddenly start praying because there was a crisis. Prayer was already part of his life. He had a pattern of faithfulness.

Daniel knew the new law could cost him his freedom—and possibly his life. But he continued to pray.

**Teaching Point:** Faithfulness isn't only what we do when it's easy. It's what we continue doing when it's hard.

### 4. Daniel Was Caught

Daniel's enemies found him praying. They went straight to the king.
King Darius realized he had been tricked. He was deeply upset. He wanted to save Daniel. But the law could not simply be changed.

Daniel had done what was right. And now he faced the consequence.
The king ordered Daniel to be thrown into the lions' den.

### 5. Daniel Entered the Lions' Den

The king said:
> **"Thy God whom thou servest continually, he will deliver thee."**

A stone was placed over the den. The king spent a sleepless night.
But Daniel was not abandoned. God sent an angel and shut the lions' mouths. Daniel was safe.

The next morning, King Darius hurried to the den. He called:
> *"Daniel, servant of the living God, is thy God, whom thou servest continually, able to deliver thee from the lions?"*

Daniel answered. He was alive. God had protected him.

### 6. Daniel Was Rescued

Daniel was lifted from the den.
The Bible tells us:
> **"So Daniel was taken up out of the den, and no manner of hurt was found upon him, because he believed in his God."**

Daniel had trusted God. God had rescued him. The king then made a proclamation honoring the living God.

## What Daniel Didn't Know

This is an important question for the class:
> **"When Daniel kept praying, did he know for certain that God would save him from the lions?"**

No.
Daniel knew God was worthy of worship. Daniel knew God was powerful. Daniel trusted God.
But Daniel did not have the ending written out in front of him. That's what makes his faithfulness meaningful. He chose obedience before knowing the outcome.

**Main Point:** Faithfulness means trusting God even when we don't know what will happen next.

## Three Things to Remember

### 1. Daniel Had a Pattern of Faithfulness
Daniel didn't suddenly decide to trust God when the lions appeared. He had already been praying. He had already been serving faithfully. He had already developed a life of trust.
*Ask: "Why do you think it helps to practice faithfulness before something difficult happens?"*

### 2. Doing Right Can Be Hard
Daniel did the right thing. And doing the right thing got him into trouble. That's important.
Sometimes we tell children: *"Do the right thing and everything will be easy."* The Bible doesn't promise that. Sometimes telling the truth is hard. Sometimes being kind is hard. Sometimes standing alone is hard. Sometimes following God means making a choice that other people don't understand.
**But: Hard does not mean wrong.**

### 3. We Can Trust God Without Knowing the Outcome
Daniel didn't know what would happen in the lions' den. But he knew God. That was enough.
We can pray: *"God, I don't know what will happen, but I trust You."* That is faith.

## Age Adaptations

<AgeGroup level="preschool">
### Younger Children

For younger children, focus on three simple truths:
1. **Daniel prayed to God.** Prayer was important to Daniel.
2. **Daniel kept doing what was right.** Even when he knew it could get him into trouble.
3. **God was with Daniel.** God protected Daniel in the lions' den.

**Simple Question:** *"Did Daniel stop loving and trusting God when things got scary?"*
**No.**

**Simple Take-Home Truth:** **"I can trust God when things are hard."**

**Important Clarification:** Don't tell younger children: *"If you pray, nothing bad will ever happen."*
Instead: *"God is with us when things are hard, and we can always talk to Him."*
</AgeGroup>

<AgeGroup level="upper-elementary">
### Older Children

Older children can explore the tension between **faithfulness and outcomes**.
Ask:
- Would Daniel still have been faithful if God had not rescued him? Why?
- Is obedience only worthwhile when we get the result we want?
- What is the difference between trusting God and assuming God will do exactly what we want?
- What does it mean to have a "pattern" of faithfulness?

**Scenario Discussion**
Give students these situations:
- A friend wants you to cheat on a test.
- Someone is being mocked and everyone expects you to laugh.
- You know you need to tell the truth even though you'll probably get in trouble.
- Your friends tell you that following God is weird.

Ask:
> *"What would faithfulness look like here?"*
Then:
> *"What might make it difficult?"*
And finally:
> *"What could you remember about God?"*
</AgeGroup>

## Discussion Questions

### Understanding
1. Why were Daniel's enemies jealous of him?
2. What law did they convince the king to make?
3. What did Daniel do when he heard about the law?
4. Where was Daniel taken?
5. What happened when Daniel was in the lions' den?

### Thinking
6. Why do you think Daniel continued his normal prayer routine?
7. Why couldn't King Darius simply change the law?
8. Did Daniel know God would rescue him?
9. What does Daniel's story teach us about faithfulness?

### Personal Application
10. When is it difficult to do the right thing?
11. Have you ever had to make a good choice when your friends wanted something different?
12. What can you do to build a pattern of faithfulness now?
13. How can you trust God when you don't know what will happen?

The verse doesn't simply say: *"Daniel was lucky."*

Daniel trusted God.

### Younger Children
Focus on: **"He believed in his God."**
Have children repeat it several times.

## Application

Ask: **"Where might you need to stand firm this week?"**
Give children examples:
- Telling the truth
- Being kind
- Refusing to cheat
- Standing up for someone
- Saying no to something wrong
- Praying
- Choosing obedience when nobody else is

Then ask: **"What makes that difficult?"**
Finally: **"What can you remember about God when doing the right thing is hard?"**

Remind them: Daniel didn't know what would happen next. But he knew God.

## Take-Home Challenge

This week, choose **one small way to practice faithfulness**.
Examples:
- Tell the truth.
- Keep a promise.
- Pray every day.
- Help someone without being asked.
- Refuse to join in when someone is being mocked.
- Admit when you've done something wrong.
- Do the right thing even when nobody is watching.

At the end of the week, ask:
> **"Where did I choose to stand firm?"**

## Closing Thought

Daniel's faith wasn't only visible in the lions' den. It had been growing through ordinary days. He served faithfully. He prayed faithfully. He trusted God faithfully. And when a difficult decision came, Daniel already knew what direction he would choose.

We don't know what difficult things we will face. But we can practice faithfulness today. We can learn to say:
> **"I don't know what will happen, but I know God is worthy of my trust."**

## Closing Prayer

When we don't know what will happen, help us trust You.

Help us remember that You are with us.
In Jesus' name,
Amen.
