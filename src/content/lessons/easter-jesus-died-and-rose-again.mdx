## Teacher Preparation

**Before class:**
- Read Luke 22–24, Matthew 27:45–28:10, and 1 Corinthians 15:1–20.
- Prepare the "The Stone Rolled Away" object lesson box, stone, and note.
- Print the "Jesus Is Alive! Tomb Craft Template" on cardstock and assemble the craft materials (paper plates, crayons, black paper).
- Write the memory verse (1 Corinthians 15:3–4) on the board.
- Prepare your heart to proclaim the gospel with solemnity and immense joy.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A note for the teacher**
> 
> **1. Jesus TRULY died.** The reality of Jesus' physical death must not be softened or glossed over. He did not faint, swoon, or slip into a coma. Roman soldiers confirmed His death, His side was pierced, and His lifeless body was wrapped in linen cloths and sealed in a stone tomb. Our sins required a real sacrifice, and Jesus paid the full penalty. Children need to understand the reality of His death so that the joy of His resurrection carries its true weight.
> 
> **2. Jesus TRULY rose bodily.** The resurrection is not a metaphor, a poetic myth, or a "warm feeling in the disciples' hearts." Jesus rose in a real, physical, flesh-and-bone body (Luke 24:39). The tomb was historically empty. He was seen by Mary Magdalene, the women, the eleven apostles, the Emmaus disciples, and more than 500 witnesses at one time (1 Corinthians 15:6).
> 
> **3. Connect Easter to Passover.** Jesus' crucifixion coincided exactly with Passover. Just as the blood of the Passover lamb saved Israel from judgment in Egypt, Jesus is "Christ our Passover lamb, who has been sacrificed" (1 Corinthians 5:7). His resurrection on the third day fulfills the Feast of Firstfruits.
> 
> **4. Cultural Easter vs. Biblical Easter.** Many children associate Easter primarily with Easter bunnies, chocolate eggs, and spring baskets. These traditions stem from secular folklore and commercial culture; they are not found in the Bible. Address this gently and respectfully—do not make children feel guilty about family fun, but clearly explain that biblical Easter is about the historic cross and empty tomb of Jesus Christ. Keep the entire focus of your lesson on the Word of God.
> 
> **5. Presenting to young children.** With preschoolers, present the crucifixion faithfully without graphic violence. Focus on the love of Jesus, the reality that He died for our sins, the quiet dark of the tomb, and the bursting morning light of the resurrection. Death is real, but in Christ, death has lost its sting!

## Opening Activity: The Mystery of the Empty Tomb

Begin by gathering the children around a small sealed box with a stone rolled against its entrance.

Ask: *"Imagine you lost something very precious, and someone put it inside a room carved out of solid rock, locked the door, and put soldiers outside to guard it. Could anyone take it away?"*

Let the children respond.

Then say: *"Two thousand years ago, the most important event in all human history took place just outside the city of Jerusalem. Jesus Christ had died on a cross, and His body was placed in a tomb sealed with a huge stone. Everyone thought the story was finished. But on the third morning, something happened that astonished the whole world."*

Invite a student to step forward and roll the stone away from the box, revealing the note inside: **"HE IS NOT HERE. HE IS RISEN."**

Today we are opening God's Word to see why Jesus died, how He was buried, and why the empty tomb means good news for each one of us!

## The Bible Story: Jesus Died and Rose Again

### 1. The Last Supper: The True Passover Lamb (Luke 22:7–20)

It was the season of Passover in Jerusalem. For over a thousand years, God's people had gathered every year to sacrifice a spotless lamb, remembering how God had rescued them out of slavery in Egypt when the angel of death passed over the homes marked with lamb's blood.

Jesus gathered His twelve disciples in an upper room for the Passover meal. But Jesus knew that this Passover was different. He was not just celebrating an old rescue; He was about to accomplish the ultimate rescue.

Jesus took bread, gave thanks, broke it, and gave it to His disciples, saying:
> *"This is My body, which is given for you; do this in remembrance of Me."* (Luke 22:19)

Then He took the cup of wine, saying:
> *"This cup is the new covenant in My blood, which is poured out for you."* (Luke 22:20)

Jesus was explaining that He Himself is the true Lamb of God. His body would be broken, and His blood would be poured out to wash away the sins of the world.

### 2. The Arrest and Trials (Luke 22:39–23:25)

Later that night, Jesus went to the Garden of Gethsemane on the Mount of Olives to pray. In deep anguish, knowing the terrible weight of sin He was about to carry, Jesus prayed:
> *"Father, if You are willing, take this cup from Me; yet not My will, but Yours be done."* (Luke 22:42)

Soon, torches lit up the trees. Judas Iscariot, one of the twelve disciples, arrived leading armed guards and betrayed Jesus with a kiss. Jesus did not fight back, nor did He call down twelve legions of angels to rescue Him. He willingly submitted to God's plan.

Jesus was dragged through the night before the Jewish high council (the Sanhedrin) and then before the Roman governor, Pontius Pilate. The religious leaders brought false accusations against Him because they were jealous of His authority. Pilate examined Jesus and stated plainly three times: *"I find no guilt in this man."*

Yet the hostile crowd shouted: *"Crucify Him! Crucify Him!"* 

Pilate gave in to the pressure of the crowd. He released Barabbas, a guilty rebel and murderer, and handed innocent Jesus over to be beaten, mocked, and crucified.

### 3. The Crucifixion: Jesus Died for Our Sins (Luke 23:33–46)

Roman soldiers placed a heavy wooden cross on Jesus and led Him to a hill called Golgotha, which means "the Place of the Skull." 

There, they nailed Jesus' hands and feet to the wooden cross and lifted Him up between two criminals. Even as He hung in excruciating pain, Jesus looked at those who mocked Him and prayed:
> *"Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they are doing."* (Luke 23:34)

One of the criminals hanging beside Jesus recognized his own guilt and looked at Jesus in faith, saying: *"Jesus, remember me when You come into Your kingdom."* Jesus replied with breathtaking mercy: *"Truly I tell you, today you will be with Me in Paradise."*

At noon, an extraordinary darkness covered the entire land for three hours. The sun stopped shining. It was as if creation itself was mourning the death of its Creator. In the temple in Jerusalem, the massive, thick curtain separating the Holy of Holies from the people was torn in two from top to bottom—showing that through Jesus' death, the barrier of sin was removed and the way to God was opened!

At three o'clock in the afternoon, Jesus cried out with a loud voice:
> *"Father, into Your hands I commit My spirit!"* (Luke 23:46)

Having said this, Jesus breathed His last. **He truly died.** A Roman centurion standing guard at the cross saw what happened and praised God, declaring: *"Surely this was a righteous man!"*

### 4. The Burial: Sealed in the Rock (Luke 23:50–56)

Evening approached quickly because the Sabbath was about to begin at sundown. A righteous member of the council named Joseph of Arimathea went boldly to Pilate and asked for the body of Jesus. 

Joseph took the body down from the cross, wrapped it reverently in a clean linen shroud, and laid Jesus in a new tomb carved out of solid rock—a tomb where no one had ever yet been laid.

A massive, circular stone was rolled into place across the entrance of the tomb. The chief priests, fearing that someone would tamper with the tomb, went to Pilate and secured an official Roman seal across the stone and stationed armed Roman guards to watch it night and day.

The disciples scattered, grief-stricken and weeping. Jesus, their beloved Master, was dead and buried.

### 5. The Empty Tomb: "He Is Not Here; He Has Risen!" (Luke 24:1–12; Matthew 28:1–10)

Friday evening passed. Saturday (the Sabbath) passed in solemn silence.

Early on Sunday morning, at the dawn of the first day of the week, several women who loved Jesus—including Mary Magdalene, Joanna, and Mary the mother of James—walked toward the tomb carrying fragrant spices to anoint Jesus' body. As they walked, they wondered among themselves: *"Who will roll away the stone for us from the entrance of the tomb?"*

Suddenly, there was a violent earthquake! An angel of the Lord descended from heaven, rolled back the massive stone, and sat upon it. His appearance was like lightning, and his clothes were white as snow. The Roman guards were so terrified of the angel that they trembled and became like dead men!

When the women arrived and looked up, they saw that the stone had already been rolled away. Stepping inside the cool tomb, they were shocked: **the body of the Lord Jesus was not there!**

Suddenly, two men in dazzling clothes stood beside them. In fear, the women bowed their faces to the ground. The angels said to them:
> *"Why do you seek the living among the dead? He is not here; He has risen! Remember how He told you, while He was still with you in Galilee: 'The Son of Man must be delivered over to the hands of sinners, be crucified and on the third day be raised again.'"* (Luke 24:5–7)

The women remembered Jesus' words! They ran with great joy and fear to tell the eleven disciples. Peter and John ran to the tomb to see for themselves. Peter looked in and saw the linen wrappings lying by themselves, but the body was gone. Peter went away wondering to himself what had happened.

### 6. The Risen Lord: Alive Forevermore (Luke 24:13–48; 1 Corinthians 15:3–6)

Jesus did not stay absent. He began appearing to His followers in bodily form!

First, He appeared to Mary Magdalene outside the garden tomb, calling her by name: *"Mary!"* She turned and cried out: *"Rabboni!"* (which means Teacher).

That same afternoon, Jesus walked alongside two disciples traveling on the road to Emmaus, opening the Scriptures to show how all the Law and Prophets pointed to His death and resurrection. When He broke bread with them that evening, their eyes were opened, and they recognized Him!

Later that night, the disciples were gathered behind locked doors because they feared the authorities. Suddenly, Jesus stood right in their midst and said:
> *"Peace be with you."* (Luke 24:36)

They were startled and terrified, thinking they were seeing a ghost. But Jesus proved His physical reality to them:
> *"Why are you troubled, and why do doubts arise in your hearts? Look at My hands and My feet, that it is I Myself! Touch Me and see, for a spirit does not have flesh and bones as you see I have."* (Luke 24:38–39)

He showed them the nail scars in His hands and feet. To remove any remaining doubt, He asked: *"Do you have anything here to eat?"* They handed Him a piece of broiled fish, and He took it and ate it right before their eyes!

This was not a spirit or a dream. Jesus was physically, bodily alive! He had conquered death once and for all. Over the next forty days, Jesus appeared to His disciples and to more than 500 people at one time (1 Corinthians 15:6), commissioning them to go into all the world and proclaim the good news of salvation.

## The Deeper Meaning: Three Things to Remember

### 1. Jesus Truly Died as Our Passover Lamb
The cross was not an accident or a tragic misunderstanding. It was God's eternal plan of redemption. Because God is holy and just, our sins deserve eternal judgment. But because God is rich in love, He sent His only Son to take our place. On the cross, Jesus bore our sins in His own body. He drank the cup of God's wrath so that we could receive the cup of God's grace. When Jesus said, *"It is finished"* (John 19:30), the debt of our sin was paid in full.

### 2. Jesus Truly Conquered Death in a Bodily Resurrection
The empty tomb is the cornerstone of the Christian faith. If Jesus had remained in the grave, our faith would be futile and we would still be in our sins (1 Corinthians 15:17). But Jesus broke the chains of death! His physical resurrection proved that His sacrifice was accepted by the Father and that death has no power over Him. Jesus holds the keys of death and Hades (Revelation 1:18).

### 3. The Resurrection Changes Everything for You and Me
Because Jesus is alive today, we do not follow a dead religious teacher—we follow a living Saviour! 
- **Forgiveness:** Your sins can be completely washed clean because Christ's blood paid the penalty.
- **Presence:** Jesus is with us right now by His Holy Spirit and will never leave us.
- **Hope:** We do not have to fear death. Because Jesus rose from the dead, all who trust in Him will one day be raised to live with Him forever in the new heavens and the new earth.

## Age Adaptations

<AgeGroup level="preschool">
### For Younger Children
For preschoolers, keep the gospel message clear, warm, and focused on three simple truths:
- **Jesus loves us and died on the cross.** (Explain gently: Jesus died to take away our wrong choices.)
- **They put Jesus in a dark tomb.** (A big heavy stone covered the door.)
- **God raised Jesus back to life on Sunday morning!** (The stone was rolled away and the tomb was empty!)

**Interactive Repetition:**
Teacher: *"Where is Jesus?"*  
Children: *"He is not here — He is alive!"*

**Visual Focus:**
Use the stone rolled away craft to let children physically move the stone back and forth, celebrating the bright sunshine and flowers outside the tomb. Remind them: *"Jesus is alive right now, and He loves you very much!"*
</AgeGroup>

<AgeGroup level="upper-elementary">
### For Older Children
With upper-elementary students, explore the historical evidence and doctrinal significance of the resurrection:
- **Historical Evidence of the Empty Tomb:**
  - *The Roman Guard and Seal:* Roman soldiers faced execution if they failed their post or allowed a body to be stolen.
  - *The Enemies' Admission:* The religious leaders did not produce a body; instead, they bribed the guards to say the disciples stole it (Matthew 28:11–15). If the body were available, the Roman and Jewish authorities could have produced it to crush Christianity immediately.
  - *The Eyewitnesses:* Over 500 witnesses saw Jesus alive at one time (1 Corinthians 15:6). You cannot have a simultaneous hallucination among 500 people.
  - *The Transformed Disciples:* Disciples who were hiding behind locked doors in terror on Friday became bold preachers willing to die as martyrs for proclaiming that they had seen Jesus alive. No one dies for something they know they invented.
- **Bodily vs. Spiritual:** Highlight Luke 24:39–43 where Jesus ate fish and showed His scars. Jesus did not return as a disembodied ghost, but in a glorified, physical human body—the prototype of our future resurrection body.
</AgeGroup>

## Discussion Questions

### Understanding
1. Why did Jesus share the bread and the cup with His disciples at the Last Supper?
2. What happened when Jesus died on the cross? (What happened to the sun and the temple curtain?)
3. Who asked for Jesus' body and buried Him in a new stone tomb?
4. What did the women see when they arrived at the tomb early on Sunday morning?
5. How did Jesus prove to His disciples that He was not a ghost or a spirit?

### Thinking
6. Why was it necessary for Jesus to truly die on the cross rather than just escape His enemies?
7. Why did the angel roll away the stone from the tomb if Jesus was already resurrected?
8. How does Jesus fulfill the Passover lamb from the Old Testament?
9. Why were the Roman guards and the religious leaders unable to keep Jesus in the tomb?

### Personal Application
10. How does knowing that Jesus truly rose from the dead give you comfort when you face fear or sadness?
11. If Jesus is alive right now, how can you talk to Him every day?
12. Who is someone in your life who needs to hear the good news that Jesus is alive?
13. What difference does the resurrection make in how you think about death?

## Memory Verse

**1 Corinthians 15:3–4 (BSB)**  
> *"For I delivered to you as of first importance what I also received: that Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures, that He was buried, that He was raised on the third day according to the Scriptures."*

For preschoolers, shorten to:  
> *"Christ died for our sins... He was raised on the third day."*

### Teach the Meaning
Explain to the students that the Apostle Paul calls this message **"of first importance."** There is nothing more important in the entire universe than this truth:
1. **Christ died for our sins** — He took our punishment so we could be forgiven.
2. **He was buried** — His death was real and complete.
3. **He was raised on the third day** — God gave Him victory over death, fulfilling all of God's promises in Scripture.

### The "Died – Buried – Raised" Method
Teach the verse with three simple, powerful physical motions:
- **"Christ died"** → Place both hands over your heart and bow your head in reverent thankfulness.
- **"He was buried"** → Slowly lower both hands downward toward the ground, palms facing down.
- **"He was raised"** → Raise both hands up high toward heaven with joyful faces!

Repeat this three-part motion together five times, reciting the words louder and with greater joy each time.

## Application

When the women met the angel at the empty tomb and heard that Jesus had risen, Matthew 28:8 tells us they departed quickly with **"fear and great joy, and ran to tell His disciples."**

The resurrection is not meant to stay inside the church walls. It is news that demands a response!

Ask the children:
- **Do you believe that Jesus died for your sins and rose again?** (You can pray right now and thank Him for paying for your sins and giving you eternal life!)
- **Are you living in joy?** When bad days come, or when you are scared of sickness or death, remember: *Jesus has conquered death.* Nothing can separate you from His love!
- **Who will you tell?** Just as the women ran to tell Peter and John, we get to tell our friends, neighbours, and family members: *"Jesus is alive!"*
