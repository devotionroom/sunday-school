## Teacher Preparation

**Before class:**
- Read the narrative arc of Joseph (Genesis 37, 39–50). 
- Pay special attention to Genesis 39:2, 39:21, and 50:20 (BSB).
- Prepare *The Big Picture Puzzle* activity (requires printing and cutting a picture beforehand).
- Write the memory verse on the board.
- Decide which age adaptation will work best for your classroom.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A note for the teacher**  
> Covering chapters 37 through 50 in a single lesson requires focusing heavily on the *theme* rather than every detail. Do not get bogged down in the mechanics of Pharaoh's dreams or the exact items put in Benjamin's sack. Keep the focus tightly on the theological through-line: **God was with Joseph, and God turned evil into good.** This is a profoundly comforting lesson for children who may be experiencing trauma, bullying, or unfairness in their own lives.

## Opening Activity: The Unfair Game

Ask the children:
*"Have you ever had a day where absolutely everything seemed unfair?"*
Allow them to share a few short examples (e.g., getting blamed for something a sibling did, losing a game because someone cheated, having a fun trip canceled).

Explain:
*"It hurts when things are unfair. Sometimes, when really unfair things happen, we might wonder if God has forgotten about us. Today, we are going to look at the life of a young man who experienced some of the most unfair things you can imagine. But we are going to see a secret hidden in his story."*

## The Bible Story

### 1. The Favorite Son and the Pit
Joseph was seventeen years old, and he had a problem: his ten older brothers hated him. Their father, Jacob, loved Joseph more than the others and gave him a beautiful, special tunic. To make matters worse, God gave Joseph dreams showing that one day, his brothers would bow down to him. 

His brothers were furious and jealous. One day, when they were far from home, they grabbed Joseph, tore off his beautiful tunic, and threw him into a deep, empty pit. They decided to sell him as a slave to some traders traveling to Egypt. 
It was completely unfair. He was dragged away to a foreign country in chains.

### 2. A Slave in Egypt
Joseph was bought by a rich Egyptian official named Potiphar. He was a slave. He had lost his family, his home, and his freedom. But the Bible tells us a very important secret:

> *"And the LORD was with Joseph, and he became a successful man..."* (Genesis 39:2, BSB)

Even as a slave, God had not left Joseph! Joseph worked hard and chose to honor God. Potiphar put Joseph in charge of his entire house. 
But then, something else unfair happened. Potiphar's wife told a terrible lie about Joseph. Even though Joseph had done exactly the right thing and ran away from sin, Potiphar believed the lie. Joseph was thrown into a dark dungeon.

### 3. The Prison and the Dreams
Now Joseph was a slave *and* a prisoner. It seemed like his life was completely ruined. But what does the Bible say?

> *"But the LORD was with Joseph and extended kindness to him..."* (Genesis 39:21, BSB)

God was in the dungeon, too! While in prison, God helped Joseph explain the meaning of dreams for two of Pharaoh's servants. One of the servants promised to remember Joseph and help him get out. But guess what? The servant forgot all about Joseph for two whole years. 
Unfair, right? But Joseph kept trusting God.

### 4. From the Dungeon to the Palace
One night, Pharaoh (the king of Egypt) had terrifying dreams about fat cows and skinny cows. No one could explain them. Finally, the servant remembered Joseph! 
They rushed Joseph out of the dungeon. Joseph told Pharaoh, *"I cannot do it, but God will give Pharaoh the answer."*

God revealed that a terrible famine (a time with no food) was coming. Because God was with Joseph, Pharaoh made Joseph the second most powerful man in all of Egypt! Joseph stored up grain and saved thousands of people from starving.

### 5. The Big Picture
Eventually, the famine reached Joseph's family. His older brothers traveled to Egypt for food. They didn't recognize Joseph, but he recognized them. 

When he finally revealed who he was, his brothers were terrified. They thought Joseph would kill them for what they did to him all those years ago. But Joseph wept, and he told them something amazing. Listen to what Joseph said:

> *"As for you, what you intended against me for evil, God intended for good, in order to accomplish a day like this—to preserve the lives of many people."* (Genesis 50:20, BSB)

Joseph finally saw the big picture. He forgave his brothers. God took the terrible, unfair, evil things his brothers did and used them to save the whole world from starvation. 

## The Deeper Meaning

Joseph's story isn't just about a guy who got lucky after a run of bad luck. It shows us how God is in control of history. Even when people do evil things, God's good plans cannot be stopped. 

Hundreds of years later, someone else would be betrayed by His friends. He would be sold for pieces of silver. He would be falsely accused and punished even though He did nothing wrong. That person was Jesus. 
When Jesus was put on the cross, it looked like the most unfair, terrible day in history. But just like Joseph's story, God was taking something intended for evil and using it for the greatest good ever: saving us from our sins. 

## Three Things to Remember

### 1. Hard Times Don't Mean God Left You
When Joseph was in the pit, the slave market, and the dungeon, the Lord was with him. When you go through hard, sad, or unfair times, God is with you, too. 
*Teacher Question:* How does it feel to know that God is with you even in the "dungeons" of life?

### 2. You Can Still Choose to Honor God
Joseph didn't use his bad circumstances as an excuse to sin. He didn't say, *"Well, my life is ruined, so I'm going to be bad."* He chose to honor God everywhere he went.
*Teacher Question:* Is it hard to make good choices when everything around you feels unfair?

### 3. God Can Turn Evil into Good
Joseph's brothers did a genuinely evil thing. But God is so powerful that He wove their bad choices into a master plan to save lives. 
*Teacher Question:* Why is it important to forgive people who hurt us?

## Age Adaptations

<AgeGroup level="preschool">
### Younger Children

For younger children (ages 5–7), focus heavily on God's constant presence. 

**Simplified concepts:**
- Joseph's brothers were mean to him and sent him far away.
- Joseph was put in a jail even though he didn't do anything wrong. 
- But God was ALWAYS with Joseph. God did not leave him.
- Eventually, God made Joseph a great leader who gave people food when they were hungry.
- Joseph forgave his brothers because he loved God.

**Developmentally appropriate questions:**
- Was God with Joseph in the dark pit? (Yes!)
- Was God with Joseph in the jail? (Yes!)
- Is God with you when you are sad or scared? (Yes!)
</AgeGroup>

<AgeGroup level="upper-elementary">
### Older Children

For older children (ages 8–11), focus on the theology of God's sovereignty and the difficulty of forgiveness.

**Deeper discussion:**
It is very hard for pre-teens to forgive people who have bullied or betrayed them. Joseph had the ultimate reason to take revenge on his brothers, but he chose forgiveness because he trusted God's sovereignty. 

**Discussion Scenarios:**
- *Scenario 1:* Someone spreads a rumor about you at school (like Potiphar's wife did to Joseph). You get in trouble. How can you respond in a way that honors God, even when it is totally unfair?
- *Scenario 2:* Read Genesis 50:20 again. Does this verse mean that what the brothers did was actually good? (No. What they did was evil. But God's power is so great that He can *use* evil things to accomplish His good purposes.)
- *Concept:* Trusting God doesn't mean we pretend bad things are good. It means we trust that God is bigger than the bad things.
</AgeGroup>

## Discussion Questions

### Understanding
1. What did Jacob give to Joseph that made his brothers jealous?
2. What did the brothers do to Joseph?
3. What did Potiphar's wife do that got Joseph thrown into the dungeon?
4. What phrase does the Bible repeat about Joseph while he was a slave and a prisoner?
5. What did Joseph do when he finally saw his brothers again years later?

### Thinking
6. Why do you think Joseph continued to obey God even when he was a slave in Egypt?
7. Did God cause the brothers to sin? (No, they chose to sin. But God used their sinful choices to move Joseph to Egypt).
8. Why did Joseph say, "what you intended against me for evil, God intended for good"?
9. How is Joseph's life a picture of what would eventually happen to Jesus?

### Personal Application
10. Have you ever felt like you were in a "pit" or a "dungeon"—a time when you felt stuck and sad?
11. How does knowing "The LORD was with Joseph" help you when you feel alone?
12. Is there someone who has been unfair to you that you need to forgive?

## Memory Verse Teaching Method

**Genesis 50:20 (BSB)**  
*"As for you, what you intended against me for evil, God intended for good..."*

**Practical Way to Teach:**
- Use hand motions to create contrast.
- *"As for you"* (point outward).
- *"what you intended against me for evil"* (cross arms tightly in front of chest, making a scowling face).
- *"God intended"* (point up to the sky).
- *"for good"* (throw arms wide open with a big smile).
- Repeat several times, speeding up the motions.

## Application

When something unfair or sad happens this week, don't use it as an excuse to make a bad choice or treat someone poorly. Instead, practice saying out loud: *"God is with me right now."* Remember that Joseph honored God even when he was sweeping floors as a slave. 

## Take-Home Challenge

If someone does something mean or unfair to you this week, choose to forgive them quickly, just like Joseph forgave his brothers. Trust that God's plan is bigger than their unfair choice.

## Closing Thought

It is easy to trust God when we are wearing a beautiful tunic and everything is going perfectly. It is much harder to trust God when we are in a dark pit. But God's promise isn't that we will never be in a pit. His promise is that He will be right there in the pit with us. He is the Master Artist, taking all the dark, ugly pieces of our lives and turning them into a beautiful masterpiece.

## Closing Prayer

"Heavenly Father, thank You that You never leave us, even when life feels unfair. Thank You for the story of Joseph, which reminds us that You can take things meant for evil and use them for good. When we go through hard times, help us to remember that You are right there with us. Give us the courage to make good choices and the strength to forgive people who hurt us. In Jesus' name, Amen."
