## Teacher Preparation

**Before class:**
- Read Matthew 2:1–12 carefully.
- Pay special attention to the words "house" (v. 11) and "young child" (v. 11).
- Prepare the Follow the Star opening activity (hide a paper star trail around the room).
- Write the memory verse somewhere visible.
- Choose an age adaptation appropriate for your group.
- If possible, bring three small gift boxes (empty) as a visual aid for gold, frankincense, and myrrh.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A note for the teacher — please read before teaching this lesson**
> The nativity scene most of us grew up with shows shepherds and wise men together at the manger on the same night. **That is a popular tradition, but it is not what the Bible describes.**
>
> The wise men's visit is recorded in **Matthew 2** — a completely different account from the shepherds' visit in **Luke 2**. Notice what Matthew tells us:
> - The wise men found Jesus in a **house** (Greek: *oikos*) — not a stable or at a manger (Matthew 2:11).
> - Jesus is called a **young child** (Greek: *paidion*) — not a newborn or infant.
> - Herod asked the Magi exactly when the star appeared and then ordered the killing of all boys **under two years old** (Matthew 2:7, 16) — which suggests the visit could have been up to two years after Jesus' birth.
>
> **The lesson is not:** *"The wise men came to the manger on Christmas night."*
> **The lesson is:** *"God drew seekers from far away to Jesus — and He still does."*
>
> You do not need to be confrontational about nativity scenes — but do be clear that this lesson is teaching **what the Bible actually says**. Children can handle the truth, and it makes the story richer.

## The Bible Story

### 1. Wise Men From the East — Who Were They?

Matthew 2:1 tells us that after Jesus was born in Bethlehem, **Magi came from the East to Jerusalem**.

Who were the Magi? They were scholars — probably astronomers and advisors to kings. The word "Magi" is where we get the English word "magician," but these were not street performers. They studied the stars and interpreted signs.

Here are some things the Bible does **not** tell us that many people assume:
- It does **not** say there were three of them. (We count three gifts, but the number of Magi is never given.)
- It does **not** call them kings. That idea comes from later Christian tradition and poetry.
- It does **not** say they were from one specific country.

What the Bible **does** tell us: They were scholars from the East, and they had seen something in the sky that pointed to the birth of a great king.

**Teaching point:** God reached people who were far from Israel and far from Jewish faith. These were Gentiles — non-Jewish people — and yet God drew them to His Son.

### 2. Following the Star

The wise men had seen **His star in the east** (Matthew 2:2).

What was this star? The Bible doesn't give us a scientific explanation. What we know is that God used it as a sign — a light pointing to the Light of the World.

The wise men made a logical first stop: **Jerusalem**, the capital city. If a king had been born in Judea, surely the palace would know about it!

So they arrived and asked: *"Where is the One who has been born King of the Jews? We saw His star in the east and have come to worship Him."*

This is our memory verse. Say it together.

### 3. The Wrong Stop — King Herod

When Herod heard this, he was **troubled** — and so was all Jerusalem with him (Matthew 2:3).

King Herod was the Roman-appointed ruler of Judea. He was known for his ruthlessness and paranoia. The idea of a rival king frightened and enraged him.

Herod called the chief priests and scribes and asked them: **"Where was the Christ to be born?"**

They answered immediately from Scripture: **Bethlehem of Judea** (Matthew 2:5–6). They quoted the prophet Micah (Micah 5:2), who had written about a ruler coming from Bethlehem centuries before.

Here is a remarkable detail: **The religious leaders knew exactly where the Messiah was to be born — but they did not go.** The wise men, foreigners who didn't even have the Scriptures, came from far away to worship. The people who had all the information stayed home.

Herod secretly called the Magi and asked exactly when the star had appeared. Then he sent them to Bethlehem — but with a sinister instruction: *"Report back to me so that I too may go and worship Him."* He had no intention of worshipping Jesus. He intended to destroy Him.

**Teaching point:** Knowing information about Jesus is not the same as seeking Him. The wise men sought. Herod schemed. Which are we?

### 4. The Right House — Worshipping Jesus

The wise men followed the star until it stopped over the place where the child was.

Matthew 2:11 says: *"On coming to the house, they saw the child with his mother Mary."*

Notice:
- **The house** — not a stable, not a manger. Some time has passed since the night of Jesus' birth.
- **The child** — the Greek word is *paidion*, used for a toddler or young child, not a newborn.

When they saw Him, **they bowed down and worshipped Him**.

Then they opened their treasures and presented three types of gifts:
- **Gold** — a gift fit for a king
- **Frankincense** — a fragrant resin used in worship and prayer
- **Myrrh** — a spice used in burial anointing (pointing, even here, to Jesus' death)

Each gift was costly. Each gift was deliberate. The wise men did not come empty-handed to meet the King.

**Teaching point:** Worship means bringing your best. The wise men gave costly things. We bring what we have — our attention, our obedience, our love.

### 5. The Warning Dream — Going Home Another Way

God did not leave the wise men without guidance.

After their visit, they were **warned in a dream** not to return to Herod (Matthew 2:12). So they went home by a different route.

God protected Jesus — and He also protected the wise men from becoming instruments of Herod's violence.

This is the same God who guided the wise men with a star and warned them in a dream. He is not a distant God who sets things in motion and walks away. He is involved. He guides. He protects.

### 6. We Can Follow Jesus Too

The story of the wise men is not just ancient history.

Their journey is a picture of **what it looks like to seek Jesus**:
- They had to make a decision to go.
- They had to keep going even when the journey was long.
- They made a wrong stop — but they corrected course.
- When they found Jesus, they worshipped with everything they had.
- They went home changed — by a different road.

**Big question:** *"Are you seeking Jesus the way the wise men did — with intention, with persistence, with your whole heart?"*

## The Deeper Meaning

### 1. Seeking Jesus Is Worth Any Journey

The wise men did not know exactly how long the journey would take. They didn't know what they would find at the end. But they kept going. Jesus is worth seeking with our whole heart, our whole attention, and all the effort we can give.

*Ask: "What might it look like to seek Jesus in everyday life?"*

### 2. God's Plan Was Always for All Nations

The wise men were Gentiles — people from outside the Jewish people. And yet they were among the first to worship the newborn King of the Jews.

This was not an accident. God had always planned to reach every nation through His Son. The wise men are an early glimpse of the gospel going to the whole world. Jesus did not come just for one group of people. He came for everyone.

*Ask: "What does it mean to you that Jesus came for people from all over the world?"*

### 3. Following Jesus Means Going Where He Leads

The wise men went home by a **different road** — because God redirected them. Following Jesus sometimes means changing direction, going an unexpected way, or leaving behind what we thought the route would look like.

The road of following Jesus is not always predictable. But it is always worth it.

## Age Adaptations

<AgeGroup level="preschool">
### Younger Children
For younger children, focus on three simple ideas:
- **A star in the sky!** God put a special star in the sky to guide the wise men to Jesus.
- **They followed!** The wise men kept following the star for a long, long time.
- **They found Jesus and said "He is the King!"** When they saw Jesus, they bowed down.

**Simple question:** *"Who did the wise men follow the star to find?"*
**Answer:** Jesus — the King!

**Simple take-home truth:** *"I can follow Jesus!"*

For younger children, don't worry about the distinction between the manger and the house — just emphasise that the wise men followed and found Jesus and worshipped Him.
</AgeGroup>

<AgeGroup level="upper-elementary">
### Older Children
Older children can engage with the historical and theological depth of this passage.

**Discussion questions for older students:**
- If the wise men weren't kings, and there might not have been three of them, why do we picture it that way? (Talk about tradition, art, and songs shaping our imagination.)
- Why do you think Herod was so threatened by the idea of a baby king?
- The religious leaders knew where the Messiah would be born but didn't go. What does that tell us about the difference between knowing and seeking?
- Why do you think God chose to reveal the birth of Jesus to Gentile scholars in a distant country?
- What does it mean that the wise men's gifts included myrrh — a burial spice?

**Challenge:**
Have students write a one-sentence response to: *"What would it look like for me to seek Jesus the way the wise men did?"*

Emphasise that following Jesus is an active choice — not a passive thing that just happens to us.
</AgeGroup>

## Discussion Questions

### Understanding
1. Where did the wise men come from?
2. What did they see in the sky?
3. Where did they go first to look for the new king?
4. What did the chief priests tell Herod about where the Messiah would be born?
5. Where did the wise men find Jesus — and what clues tell us it was not on the night of His birth?
6. What three gifts did they bring?

### Thinking
7. Why do you think Herod was troubled when he heard about the birth of a new king?
8. The religious leaders knew the answer from Scripture but did not go to Bethlehem. What does that tell us?
9. Why do you think God warned the wise men in a dream not to go back to Herod?
10. The wise men were from a foreign country and not part of the Jewish people. What does their worship of Jesus tell us about who Jesus came for?

### Personal Application
11. The wise men sought Jesus intentionally, over a long distance. What does it look like to seek Jesus in everyday life?
12. The wise men brought their best gifts. What can we bring to Jesus?
13. Following Jesus meant going home by a different road. Can you think of a time when following God meant changing direction?

## Memory Verse

**Matthew 2:2 (BSB)**
> *"Where is the One who has been born King of the Jews? We saw His star in the east and have come to worship Him."*

### Teach the Meaning
This is the question the wise men asked when they arrived in Jerusalem. It tells us everything about their purpose: they were not tourists. They were not on a detour. They had come specifically and intentionally **to worship the King**.

Ask: *"What does it tell us about the wise men that they travelled so far just to worship?"*

### Teaching Method — "Where Is the King?"

**Instructions:**
1. The teacher asks: *"Where is the King?"* while pointing to different places in the room — the door, the window, a chair, the ceiling.
2. Each time, children respond together: *"Not here!"*
3. Finally the teacher points to a small nativity picture or manger scene.
4. Everyone says together: *"HERE! The King is here — in a humble place!"*

Then teach the verse phrase by phrase, adding one at a time:
- *"Where is the One who has been born King of the Jews?"*
- *"We saw His star in the east…"*
- *"…and have come to worship Him."*

Repeat the whole verse together three times.

For younger children: Focus on the last phrase — **"We have come to worship Him"** — and have children repeat it with hands raised.

## Application

Ask: **"The wise men made a very long journey to find Jesus. What journey are you on with Jesus right now?"**

Give children time to think quietly.

Then ask: **"Is there something that is making it hard to follow Jesus — a distraction, a fear, a question?"**

Remind them: The wise men didn't have a perfect journey. They made a wrong stop. But they corrected course and kept going. **We can too.**

When something distracts us from following Jesus, we can ask: *"Where is the King? Let me find my way back."*

## Take-Home Challenge

This week, choose **one way to seek Jesus intentionally**:

### LOOK
Notice something God has made — the sky, a tree, something beautiful — and remember that He is the God who put a star in the sky to draw people to His Son.

### LISTEN
Read Matthew 2:1–12 for yourself. What detail do you notice that you hadn't thought about before?

### FOLLOW
Choose one thing you know Jesus wants you to do — be kind to someone, tell the truth, forgive someone — and do it, even if it's hard.

A simple prayer:
> **"Jesus, You are the King. Help me seek You the way the wise men did — with my whole heart. Lead me where You want me to go."**

## Closing Thought

The wise men saw a light in the sky and followed it across a great distance. They made a wrong stop. They were warned in a dream. They went home by a different road.

But in the end, they found Jesus. They bowed down. They worshipped.

Their story is a picture of every person who seeks Jesus with their whole heart.

The journey isn't always straightforward. Sometimes we make a wrong stop. Sometimes the road takes an unexpected turn. But **the King is worth finding**. And the God who put a star in the sky to guide strangers from a distant land is the same God who guides us today.

He is still leading people to His Son.

**Are you following?**

## Closing Prayer

Father, thank You for the story of the wise men — people from far away who You drew to Your Son.

Thank You that Jesus came not just for one people, but for all people. Thank You that You still draw seekers to Yourself.

Help us to seek Jesus the way the wise men did — with intention, with persistence, with our whole hearts.

When the road is long, help us keep following.
When we make a wrong stop, help us correct course.
And when we find You, help us worship with everything we have.

Thank You that the journey is always worth it.

In Jesus' name,
