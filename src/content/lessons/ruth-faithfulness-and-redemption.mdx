## Teacher Preparation

**Before class:**
- Read the Book of Ruth (BSB). It is only 4 chapters and reads like a beautiful short story.
- Familiarize yourself with the concept of the *Kinsman-Redeemer* (a close relative who had the legal right to buy back land that a poor relative had lost, and to marry a childless widow to continue the family name).
- Prepare the materials (tape, cotton balls/paper) for *The Gleaning Game*.
- Write the memory verse on the board.
- Decide which age adaptation will work best for your classroom.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A note for the teacher**
> The book of Ruth is a masterclass in how God works through ordinary, faithful people. There are no burning bushes, parted seas, or booming voices from heaven in this story. God's providence is seen through the quiet, everyday kindness of Ruth and Boaz. Emphasize to the children that they don't have to do something "famous" to be used by God; small acts of kindness and loyalty are powerful in God's hands.

## Opening Activity: Leaving Everything Behind

Ask the children:
*"If you had to move to a brand new country tomorrow, and you could only pack one small backpack, what would you bring?"*

Allow the children to share a few answers. Then ask:
*"What would you miss the most about your home?"*

Explain:
*"Moving far away is really hard, especially if you have to leave your friends and your home behind. Today, we are going to learn about a woman who left her country, her friends, and everything she knew behind, just because she wanted to take care of someone she loved."*

## The Bible Story

### 1. Tragedy in Moab
During a terrible famine in Israel, a man named Elimelech took his wife, Naomi, and their two sons to live in a foreign country called Moab. While they were there, the two sons married Moabite women named Orpah and Ruth.

But then, terrible tragedy struck. Naomi's husband died. Then, ten years later, both of her sons died too. In the ancient world, if a woman had no husband and no sons, she had no way to make money or protect herself. Naomi was heartbroken, poor, and alone.

Naomi decided to travel back to her home in Bethlehem (in Israel) because she heard the famine was over. She told her two daughters-in-law to stay in Moab and find new husbands. Orpah cried and kissed Naomi goodbye. But Ruth refused to leave.

### 2. Ruth's Choice
Ruth made one of the most beautiful promises in the entire Bible. She clung to Naomi and said:
> *"Do not urge me to leave you or to turn back from following you. For where you go, I will go, and where you stay, I will stay. Your people will be my people, and your God will be my God."* (Ruth 1:16, BSB)

Ruth chose to leave her own country to follow the one true God, and to take care of Naomi. So the two widows traveled back to Bethlehem together, arriving right at the beginning of the barley harvest.

### 3. Gleaning in the Fields
Naomi and Ruth were very poor and had no food. But God had given the Israelites a special law. Farmers were not allowed to harvest the very edges of their fields, and they couldn't pick up the grain they dropped. They had to leave it for the poor people to gather. This was called "gleaning."

Ruth went out to glean, and she "happened" to end up in a field belonging to a wealthy, kind man named Boaz. 

When Boaz saw Ruth working so hard, he asked who she was. His workers told him how she had left her homeland to take care of Naomi. Boaz was amazed by her loyalty. He told Ruth to stay in his fields where she would be safe, and he even secretly told his workers to drop extra grain on purpose for her to find!

### 4. The Kinsman-Redeemer
When Ruth came home with massive amounts of grain, Naomi was overjoyed. She realized that Boaz was a close relative of her dead husband. In Israel, a close relative could be a **"Kinsman-Redeemer."** This was a family hero who could step in, pay off debts, buy back lost land, and rescue a widow.

Naomi told Ruth to go to the threshing floor at night, uncover Boaz's feet while he slept, and lie down. This sounds strange to us, but it was an ancient, respectful way of asking him to spread his blanket over her—meaning, asking him to protect her and marry her.

When Boaz woke up, Ruth said:
> *"Spread the corner of your garment over your maidservant, for you are a kinsman-redeemer."* (Ruth 3:9, BSB)

Boaz was honored. He promised to do everything he could to rescue them. 

### 5. The Big Surprise
Boaz went to the town gate and legally bought back all the land that belonged to Naomi's family. He became the Kinsman-Redeemer, and he took Ruth to be his wife. God blessed them with a baby boy named Obed. Naomi, who had been so sad and empty, was full of joy again as she held her new grandson.

But the story doesn't end there! The Bible gives us a big surprise at the very end of the book. 
Obed grew up and had a son named Jesse. And Jesse had a son named... **David**.
Ruth, the poor, foreign widow who left everything to be faithful to God, became the great-grandmother of King David! And hundreds of years later, Jesus Christ Himself would be born into this exact same family.

## The Deeper Meaning

The concept of the **Kinsman-Redeemer** is a perfect picture of Jesus. To be a Kinsman-Redeemer, a man had to meet three requirements:
1. **He had to be a relative (Kinsman).**
2. **He had to have the ability to pay the debt.**
3. **He had to be willing to do it.**

Look at how Jesus is our ultimate Redeemer!
1. **He became our relative:** Jesus left heaven and became a human being so He could be one of us.
2. **He had the ability to pay:** Because Jesus lived a perfect, sinless life, only He could pay the debt for our sins.
3. **He was willing to do it:** Jesus willingly laid down His life on the cross because He loves us.

Boaz redeemed Ruth from poverty. Jesus redeemed us from sin!

## Three Things to Remember

### 1. Loyalty is Powerful
Ruth could have taken the easy way out and stayed in Moab, but she chose the hard, faithful path. God loves when we are loyal to our friends, our family, and to Him.
*Teacher Question:* When is it hard to be a loyal friend?

### 2. God Works Through Small Kindness
Boaz didn't have to leave extra grain for Ruth, but he chose to be generous. God used Boaz's small acts of kindness to literally save Naomi and Ruth from starving.
*Teacher Question:* What is a small act of kindness you could do this week that might be a big deal to someone else?

### 3. Jesus is Our Redeemer
Just like Ruth and Naomi couldn't rescue themselves from their poverty, we cannot rescue ourselves from sin. We need a Redeemer, and Jesus is the perfect one.
*Teacher Question:* Why did Jesus have to become a human being to save us?

## Age Adaptations

<AgeGroup level="preschool">
### Younger Children

For younger children (ages 5–7), focus on Ruth's loyalty and Boaz's kindness.

**Simplified concepts:**
- Naomi was very sad because her family died and she had no food.
- Ruth was a good, loyal friend. She promised to stay with Naomi forever.
- Ruth went to pick up leftover food in a field. 
- A nice man named Boaz owned the field. He shared his food and protected Ruth.
- God used Boaz to rescue Naomi and Ruth, and eventually, Ruth had a baby who was in the same family as Jesus!

**Developmentally appropriate questions:**
- Did Ruth leave Naomi when things got sad and hard? (No).
- Was Boaz mean and greedy, or nice and generous? (Nice and generous).
- Who takes care of us and rescues us? (Jesus).
</AgeGroup>

<AgeGroup level="upper-elementary">
### Older Children

For older children (ages 8–11), focus heavily on the theological concept of the Kinsman-Redeemer.

**Deeper discussion:**
Help pre-teens understand the legal/historical concept of the Kinsman-Redeemer so they can fully grasp the beauty of what Jesus did on the cross.

**Discussion Scenarios:**
- *Scenario 1:* Imagine you owe a million dollars and you have zero in your bank account. If your best friend wants to help you, but they also have zero dollars, can they redeem (rescue) you? (No, they lack the *ability*). What if a billionaire stranger comes to town, but he doesn't care about you? (He lacks the *willingness*). Jesus had both! 
- *Concept:* Notice that Ruth was a Moabitess (a foreigner). She wasn't an Israelite. By making her the great-grandmother of King David, God was proving that His love and salvation are for *everyone*, not just one specific group of people.
</AgeGroup>

## Discussion Questions

### Understanding
1. Why did Naomi and her family move to Moab in the first place?
2. What did Ruth tell Naomi when Naomi told her to go back home?
3. What is "gleaning"?
4. What nice things did Boaz do for Ruth when she was in his field?
5. Who was Ruth's famous great-grandson?

### Thinking
6. Why was it dangerous and difficult for Ruth to go to Bethlehem with Naomi?
7. Do you think it was a "coincidence" that Ruth ended up in Boaz's field? Why or why not?
8. What were the three things a Kinsman-Redeemer had to be? (A relative, able to pay, willing).
9. How is Jesus like Boaz? How are we like Ruth?

### Personal Application
10. Have you ever had a friend stick with you when you were going through a hard time? How did it feel?
11. How can you show the kind of loyalty that Ruth showed?
12. Why does knowing that Jesus is your Redeemer make you want to follow Him?

## Application

Being loyal doesn't just mean sticking around when things are fun. This week, look for a way to be a loyal friend when it's hard. If someone is sitting alone, sit with them. If someone is being made fun of, stand up for them. Choose to be a faithful friend like Ruth.

## Take-Home Challenge

Boaz dropped extra grain on purpose so Ruth would find it. This week, try to do a "secret kindness" for someone in your family. Drop a little "extra grain" (do a chore without being asked, leave a nice note) and see what happens!

## Closing Thought

The book of Ruth shows us that God uses ordinary people who make faithful choices. You might not feel like a king or a superhero, but when you are kind, loyal, and faithful to God, He uses your life to build a beautiful story. And best of all, the book of Ruth points us to Jesus—our perfect Redeemer who loved us enough to rescue us when we had nothing to offer Him in return.

## Closing Prayer

"Heavenly Father, thank You for the beautiful story of Ruth. Thank You for showing us what true loyalty and kindness look like. But most of all, thank You for sending Jesus to be our perfect Kinsman-Redeemer. Thank You that Jesus came to rescue us from our sins. Help us to be loyal friends and to show Your kindness to the people around us this week. In Jesus' name, Amen."
