## Teacher Preparation

**Before class:**
- Read Luke 15:11–32 in your Bible.
- Reflect on the cultural gravity: an ancient Middle Eastern father pulling up his robes to run was considered deeply humbling. The father willingly took on public humiliation to shield his son from the shame of the community.
- Prepare the backpack and robe for the *Welcome Home Relay*.
- Print the *Father Welcomes His Son* template and prepare blue construction paper for each child.
- Write the memory verse clearly where children can see it.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A note for the teacher**
> Be careful not to teach this story as though sin does not matter because "dad will just fix it." The son suffered real hunger, isolation, and shame because of his rebellion. The true heart of the parable is the **character of the Father**. Furthermore, do not skip the older brother: remind children that obeying rules with a bitter, self-righteous, and unloving heart is just as far from God as running to a distant country.

## Opening: What Is Lost and Found?

Ask the children:
*"Have you ever lost something that was precious to you—like a favorite toy, a pet, or a treasured jacket? How did you feel while looking for it? And how did you feel the moment you found it?"*

Allow 2–3 children to share.

Explain:
*"Finding a lost toy makes us happy. Finding a lost pet brings tears of joy. But in today's story, Jesus tells a parable about something far more valuable than a toy or a pet. It is the story of a lost child, and a father whose love never stopped waiting for him to come home."*

## The Bible Story: Luke 15:11–32

### 1. The Disrespectful Request
Jesus said: *"There was a man who had two sons."* One day, the younger son came to his father with a shocking demand: *"Father, give me the share of the estate that falls to me."*

In that culture, an inheritance was only given after a father passed away. Asking for it while his father was still alive was like saying, "I care more about your money than I care about you. I wish you were dead." Yet, in astonishing patience and generosity, the father divided his wealth between his two sons.

### 2. The Distant Country and the Pig Pen
Not long after, the younger son packed everything he owned and traveled to a distant country. Far away from his father's house, his rules, and his love, the son squandered all his wealth on reckless, wild living.

Then disaster struck. A severe famine swept across the land. The son had spent every single coin. He had no food, no home, and no friends. Desperate to survive, he hired himself out to a local citizen who sent him into the fields to feed pigs. The son was so hungry that he longed to fill his stomach with the carob pods the pigs were eating, but no one gave him anything.

### 3. Coming to His Senses
Sitting in the mud, surrounded by pigs, the young man finally came to his senses. He remembered what his father's house was really like.

He thought to himself:
> *"How many of my father’s hired servants have food to spare, and here I am starving to death! I will set out and go back to my father and say to him: Father, I have sinned against heaven and against you. I am no longer worthy to be called your son; make me like one of your hired servants."* (Luke 15:17–19, BSB)

He wasn't making excuses. He admitted his sin, turned his back on the pig pen, and started the long walk home.

### 4. The Father Runs
Day after day, the father had been looking down that road, watching and waiting. 

As the son trudged down the path, barefoot, dirty, and covered in farm mud, the father saw him while he was still a long way off. The father did not wait with crossed arms. He was filled with deep compassion! He pulled up his long robes and **ran** down the road, threw his arms around his son's neck, and kissed him.

The son began his rehearsed speech: *"Father, I have sinned against heaven and against you. I am no longer worthy to be called your son..."*

### 5. Restored as a Son
The father didn't even let him finish the sentence about being a hired worker! Instead, he shouted to his servants:
> *"Quick! Bring the best robe and put it on him. Put a ring on his finger and sandals on his feet. Bring the fattened calf and kill it. Let’s have a feast and celebrate. For this son of mine was dead and is alive again; he was lost and is found!"* (Luke 15:22–24, BSB)

Every gift had deep meaning:
- **The Best Robe:** Covered his dirty rags with honor and royal dignity.
- **The Ring:** Proved he had authority and full membership in the family again.
- **The Sandals:** Slaves went barefoot in the house; sons wore sandals!

### 6. The Older Brother's Bitter Heart
Meanwhile, the older brother was working hard out in the field. As he came near the house, he heard music and dancing. When a servant told him his brother had returned safe and sound and his father had thrown a feast, the older brother was furious and refused to go inside.

The father came outside and tenderly pleaded with him. The older son complained: *"All these years I have slaved for you and never disobeyed an order, yet you never gave me a feast with my friends!"*

The father spoke with gentleness:
> *"My son, you are always with me, and everything I have is yours. But we had to celebrate and be glad, because this brother of yours was dead and is alive again; he was lost and is found."* (Luke 15:31–32, BSB)

The father loved both sons—the runaway rebel and the proud, bitter worker.

## The Deeper Meaning: Three Things to Remember

### 1. You Can Always Come Home to God
Sin promises freedom, but it always leads to the pig pen of regret. The good news of the gospel is that no matter how far you have wandered or how badly you have failed, God's door is never locked against a repentant heart.

### 2. God's Love Runs to Meet Us
Notice that the father didn't make his son stand on probation for six months to see if he had changed. When we turn to God in true repentance, He washes away our guilt immediately and clothes us in Christ's righteousness.

### 3. Beware of Older-Brother Pride
The older brother was physically close to the father, but his heart was far away. He thought he deserved the father's love because of his hard work. We cannot earn God's love, and we must never look down on others who receive God's mercy.

## Age Adaptations

<AgeGroup level="preschool">
### Younger Children (Ages 5–7)
- **Focus:** The father's loving embrace.
- **Key Idea:** When you say "I'm sorry" to God, He doesn't stay angry. He wraps His arms around you and loves you.
- **Action:** Have children practice running in place and giving themselves a giant hug when the father meets the son.
</AgeGroup>

<AgeGroup level="upper-elementary">
### Older Children (Ages 8–11)
- **Focus:** The contrast between legalism (the older son) and grace (the father).
- **Key Idea:** Discuss why doing the right things on the outside while holding bitterness, jealousy, and pride on the inside is just as dangerous as running away.
- **Challenge:** Ask: *"Have you ever felt annoyed when someone who did something wrong was forgiven quickly? How does Jesus' story challenge our selfish hearts?"*
</AgeGroup>

## Discussion Questions

### Understanding the Story
1. Why was it disrespectful for the younger son to ask for his inheritance early?
2. What happened to the younger son when his money ran out in the distant country?
3. What did the son say to himself while feeding the pigs?
4. What did the father do the moment he spotted his son walking down the road?
5. What three special gifts did the father give his son, and what did they mean?

### Thinking Deeper
6. Why did the father run to his son instead of waiting for him on the front porch?
7. What was wrong with the older brother's attitude toward his brother's return?
8. Did the younger son earn his way back into the family? How does this teach us about God's grace?

### Personal Application
9. Have you ever felt too embarrassed or ashamed to tell your parents or God that you made a mistake?
10. What does this story tell us we should do whenever we make a bad choice?
11. How can you show welcoming kindness to someone this week who might feel left out or unloved?

## Memory Verse

**Luke 15:24 (BSB)**  
> *"For this son of mine was dead and is alive again; he was lost and is found."*

### Teaching Method: The Stand-Up Echo
1. Have all children sit on the floor with heads bowed for the first part: *"For this son of mine was dead..."*
2. On *"and is alive again!"*, everyone jumps to their feet with hands stretched high!
3. For *"he was lost..."*, everyone shields their eyes with one hand looking left and right.
4. On *"and is found!"*, everyone claps their hands together and cheers with a joyful smile!
Repeat three times until the verse is memorized. Play the Scripture song during craft and snack time.

## Application

When we make a bad choice, Satan whispers: *"Stay away from God. He's furious with you. You're not worthy."* But Jesus told this story to show us the exact opposite. 

God doesn't want you hiding in the pig pen. When you realize you've done wrong, turn around! Come to God in prayer and say: *"Father, I'm sorry. Forgive me."* You will find a Father whose arms are already wide open to welcome you, forgive you, and restore you.

---

## Take-Home Challenge

This week, practice the **"Turn Around" Rule**:
- Whenever you catch yourself making a bad choice (like yelling at a sibling, whining, or telling a lie), **STOP immediately**.
- Turn around physically, whisper a quick prayer: *"Father, I'm sorry. Help me do what is right,"* and choose the right path right then.

---

## Closing Thought

God is not waiting for an excuse to push you away; He is looking down the road, waiting to welcome you with open arms.

Help us celebrate Your mercy and show that same welcoming love to everyone we meet. In Jesus' name, Amen."*
