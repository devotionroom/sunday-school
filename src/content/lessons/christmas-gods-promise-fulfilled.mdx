## Teacher Preparation

**Before class:**
- Read Isaiah 7:14; Isaiah 9:6; Micah 5:2; Luke 1:26–38; Matthew 1:18–25; Luke 2:1–20.
- Pay special attention to the specific prophecies and how each was fulfilled.
- Prepare the Promise Chain activity — write one prophecy on each paper strip before class.
- Pre-cut Christmas Star Templates for preschoolers and younger children.
- Write the memory verse somewhere visible.
- Choose the age adaptation appropriate for your group.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A note for the teacher**
> Christmas is often taught as a warm, familiar story — and it is. But it is far more than a nativity scene.
> The birth of Jesus is the fulfilment of hundreds of years of specific, detailed promises that God made through the prophets.
> The manger was not an accident or a setback. It was **exactly** where God said the Saviour would appear — in humility, in Bethlehem, born of a virgin.
> Help children see that the Christmas story is not just a nice story. It is **God keeping His word** — perfectly, precisely, at the exact right time.
> Avoid presenting Christmas as primarily about gifts, feelings, or tradition. The heart of the lesson is: **God promised. God kept His promise. That changes everything.**

## The Bible Story

### 1. The Long Wait

Hundreds of years before Jesus was born, God spoke through prophets — messengers who carried His words to His people.

The prophet Isaiah wrote:
> *"The virgin will conceive and give birth to a son, and will call him Immanuel."* — Isaiah 7:14

The prophet Micah wrote:
> *"But you, Bethlehem Ephrathah, though you are small among the clans of Judah, out of you will come for me one who will be ruler over Israel."* — Micah 5:2

Isaiah wrote again:
> *"For to us a child is born, to us a son is given... And he will be called Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace."* — Isaiah 9:6

God's people waited. And waited. Generations came and went. Many wondered: *Will God really keep His promise?*

**Teaching Point:** God's people waited a long time — but God had not forgotten. He was working out His plan at exactly the right time.

### 2. The Angel's Announcement

Then, in the town of Nazareth, an angel named Gabriel appeared to a young woman named Mary.

Gabriel told her:
> *"You will conceive and give birth to a son, and you are to call him Jesus. He will be great and will be called the Son of the Most High."* — Luke 1:31–32

Mary was amazed. She asked how this could be, since she had never been with a man.

Gabriel told her: *"The Holy Spirit will come on you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you."* — Luke 1:35

Mary responded with faith: *"I am the Lord's servant. May your word to me be fulfilled."* — Luke 1:38

Meanwhile, Joseph — the man Mary was engaged to — was troubled when he heard she was going to have a child. But an angel appeared to him in a dream and said:
> *"Joseph son of David, do not be afraid to take Mary home as your wife, because what is conceived in her is from the Holy Spirit. She will give birth to a son, and you are to give him the name Jesus, because he will save his people from their sins."* — Matthew 1:20–21

Matthew tells us this happened to fulfil Isaiah's prophecy: *"The virgin will conceive and give birth to a son, and they will call him Immanuel."* — Matthew 1:23

**Teaching Point:** God did not keep His promise in secret. He announced it — through an angel, to ordinary people who trusted Him.

### 3. The Journey to Bethlehem

Around this time, Caesar Augustus — the ruler of Rome — ordered a census. Everyone had to travel to their hometown to register.

Joseph was from Bethlehem, the city of David. So he and Mary travelled there — even though Mary was soon to give birth.

When they arrived, there was no room for them in the inn.

So Jesus was born in the humblest of places.
Mary wrapped Him in cloths and placed Him in a manger — a feeding trough for animals.

*Ask: "Does that sound like the way you would expect a king to arrive?"*

But remember Micah's prophecy: *"Out of you, Bethlehem, will come one who will be ruler over Israel."* God had said it would be Bethlehem. And it was.

**Teaching Point:** The manger was not a setback. It was part of God's perfect plan — exactly where He said, exactly how He said.

### 4. The Shepherds

That same night, in fields outside Bethlehem, shepherds were watching over their flocks.

Suddenly, an angel of the Lord appeared to them. The glory of the Lord shone around them — and they were terrified.

But the angel said:
> *"Do not be afraid. I bring you good news that will cause great joy for all the people. Today in the city of David a Savior has been born to you. He is the Messiah, the Lord. This will be a sign to you: You will find a baby wrapped in cloths and lying in a manger."* — Luke 2:10–12

Then a great company of angels appeared — praising God:
> *"Glory to God in the highest heaven, and on earth peace to those on whom his favour rests."* — Luke 2:14

When the angels left, the shepherds said to one another: *"Let's go to Bethlehem and see this thing that has happened, which the Lord has told us about."* — Luke 2:15

They hurried. And they found Mary, Joseph, and the baby lying in the manger.

After seeing Jesus, the shepherds went out and told everyone what had happened. And they returned to their fields glorifying and praising God.

**Teaching Point:** God announced the birth of His Son first — not to kings or priests or powerful people — but to ordinary shepherds in a field. No one is too ordinary to receive God's good news.

### 5. God Keeps His Word

Look back at every promise:

- *Born of a virgin?* ✓ — Mary had never been with a man.
- *Born in Bethlehem?* ✓ — The census brought them there.
- *Called Emmanuel — God with us?* ✓ — God had come to live among His people.
- *A Saviour born for all people?* ✓ — The angel said: *"good news... for all the people."*

Every detail. Every promise. Every word God spoke — fulfilled.

**Main Point:** God always keeps His promises. He kept them at Christmas — and we can trust Him to keep every promise He has ever made.

## Three Things to Remember

### 1. God's Promises Are Specific
God did not say vaguely, *"One day I will help you."* He said exactly where the Saviour would be born, exactly how He would come, and exactly what He would do. When we see how specifically those promises came true, we know we can trust every word God says.

### 2. God's Timing Is Perfect
Hundreds of years of waiting felt like a long time to God's people. But God sent Jesus *"when the fullness of time had come"* (Galatians 4:4). The wait was not a mistake. God knew exactly the right moment. When we are waiting for God to work, we can remember: He is never late.

### 3. God's Plan Looked Different Than Expected
Nobody expected the Saviour to arrive in a manger. No palace. No fanfare for kings. Just a stable, some shepherds, and a baby. God's ways are often surprising — but they are always better than what we would have planned. The manger was not a plan B. It was always plan A.

## Age Adaptations

<AgeGroup level="preschool">
### Younger Children
For younger children, simplify the lesson to three big ideas:
- **God promised to send someone special.** God made a promise a long, long time ago.
- **Jesus was born in Bethlehem.** That is exactly what God said would happen!
- **Angels told the shepherds.** The shepherds were so happy, they ran to see Jesus!

**Simple Question:** *"Did God keep His promise?"*
**Answer:** Yes! He always does!

**Simple Take-Home Truth:** *"God keeps His promises — even at Christmas!"*

Use the Promise Chain with pictures instead of words. Let each child hold a star while you tell the story.
</AgeGroup>

<AgeGroup level="upper-elementary">
### Older Children
Older children can explore the deeper significance of prophecy and fulfilment.

Ask:
- Why do you think God gave so many specific prophecies hundreds of years in advance?
- What would it mean if even one prophecy had not come true?
- Why do you think God chose Bethlehem? Why a manger?
- The shepherds were among the least important people in society. Why do you think God announced the birth to them first?
- What does God's faithfulness in keeping His Christmas promises tell us about how He will keep His promises to us?

**Challenge:**
Have students look up Micah 5:2 and Isaiah 7:14. Ask: *"These were written hundreds of years before Jesus was born. What would it take for someone to invent that kind of detail after the fact?"*

Emphasise: the fulfilment of prophecy is evidence that the Bible is not a human invention — it is the Word of God.
</AgeGroup>

## Discussion Questions

### Understanding
1. What did the prophet Isaiah say about how the Saviour would be born?
2. Where did the prophet Micah say the Saviour would come from?
3. Why did Mary and Joseph travel to Bethlehem?
4. Where was Jesus born, and why?
5. Who did the angel appear to first with the news of Jesus' birth?

### Thinking
6. Why do you think God gave so many specific promises before Jesus was born?
7. The shepherds were ordinary, working people. Why do you think God chose them to hear the news first?
8. How do you think Mary and Joseph felt on the journey to Bethlehem, knowing what was about to happen?
9. The shepherds *hurried* to Bethlehem after the angels left. What does that tell us about how they responded to God's news?

### Personal Application
10. God kept His promises at Christmas, even after hundreds of years. What does that tell you about whether you can trust Him with your own life?
11. Is there something you are waiting for God to do? How does the Christmas story help you to keep trusting?
12. How can you share the message of Christmas — that God keeps His promises — with someone this week?

## Memory Verse

**Luke 2:11 (BSB)**
> *"Today in the city of David a Savior has been born to you. He is Christ the Lord."*

### Teach the Meaning
Ask: *"Who is this verse talking about?"* (Jesus.)
Ask: *"Where was Jesus born?"* (The city of David — Bethlehem.)
Ask: *"What does it mean that He is Christ the Lord?"* (He is the promised Saviour — the King God promised to send.)

### Memory Verse Hand Motions
Teach the verse phrase by phrase, adding one at a time:

- **"Today in the city of David"** — point downward at the ground (Bethlehem is right here!)
- **"a Savior has been born to you"** — spread both arms wide open (a gift for everyone!)
- **"He is Christ the Lord"** — point upward with one finger (He is Lord over all!)

Repeat the full verse together three times, adding motions each time. For younger children, focus on just the first phrase and the hand motion.

## Application

Ask: **Has God ever kept a promise to you, or to someone you know?**
Give children a moment to think or share.

Then say: *"God made dozens of very specific promises about how the Saviour would come. Every single one came true at Christmas."*

Ask: **What does that tell you about whether you can trust God's promises to you?**

Remind them:
- God promised. ✓
- God kept His promise. ✓
- He will always keep His promises.

The manger reminds us: **God's promises do not fail. They may surprise us. They may come in ways we didn't expect. But they never fail.**

## Take-Home Challenge

This week, find one promise God has made in the Bible. Write it on a strip of paper and add it to a mini promise chain of your own.

### FIND IT
Look up one of these promises (or choose your own):
- *"I will never leave you nor forsake you."* — Hebrews 13:5
- *"Cast all your anxiety on him because he cares for you."* — 1 Peter 5:7
- *"For God so loved the world that he gave his one and only Son."* — John 3:16

### WRITE IT
Write the verse on a strip of paper.

### HOLD IT
When something feels uncertain or scary this week, hold the strip and remember: **God always keeps His promises.**

### TELL IT
Tell one person — a parent, friend, or sibling — something you learned today about how God kept His promise at Christmas.

## Closing Thought

Hundreds of years passed.
Generations of God's people waited.
Prophets spoke words they did not fully understand.
And then — in a stable in Bethlehem, on an ordinary night — everything changed.

God kept every promise.
In a manger.
To shepherds.
Through a young mother who said: *"May your word to me be fulfilled."*

The Christmas story is not just a beautiful story.
It is proof that **God keeps His word.**
And if God kept His word then, we can trust Him to keep every promise He has made to us.

## Closing Prayer

Father, thank You for keeping Your promises.
Thank You that Christmas is not just a story — it is proof that You do what You say.
You promised a Saviour, and You sent Him.
You promised He would be born in Bethlehem, and He was.
You announced His birth to ordinary shepherds, because Your good news is for everyone.
Help us to trust Your promises the way those shepherds trusted the angel's news — with urgency, with joy, and with praise.
Thank You for the greatest promise of all: Jesus.
In His name,
Amen.
