## Teacher Preparation

**Before class:**
- Read Exodus 11–14; 1 Corinthians 5:7; John 1:29.
- Prepare the 10 paper slips labelled "LEAVEN" for the opening search activity and hide them around the room.
- Print the "God Makes a Way! Craft Template" onto white cardstock (pre-cut pieces for younger children).
- Gather blue construction paper, crayons/markers (blue and dark blue), white chalk or crayons, scissors, and glue sticks.
- Draw a simple outline of a door frame with lintel and doorposts on a whiteboard or paper to use during the memory verse time.
- Review the theological connections between the Passover lamb and Christ.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A note for the teacher**
> Passover is one of the most foundational events in the entire Bible. As you teach, keep these vital truths in mind:
> 1. **Salvation is by grace, not human effort:** The Israelites did not earn their rescue. Placing blood on the doorposts was an act of faith and humble obedience to God's word. It was God who passed over, God who broke Pharaoh's grip, and God who parted the waters. Never suggest the Israelites were saved because they were better or harder-working than the Egyptians.
> 2. **Passover vs. Feast of Unleavened Bread:** The Passover was the specific night of the tenth plague and the midnight exodus; the seven-day Feast of Unleavened Bread began immediately following. They ate unleavened bread in haste because their departure was so sudden they had no time to let dough rise (Exodus 12:34, 39). Distinguish between the two accurately without blurring them together.
> 3. **Sensitivity with the tenth plague:** The death of the firstborn is a solemn, weighty reality showing the severity of God's judgment on Pharaoh's defiant rebellion. Present it reverently and honestly: Pharaoh repeatedly hardened his heart and refused God's command. God provided a way of escape for all who would trust His word.
> 4. **Sequential history:** Teach the events in their biblical order: Passover night → hasty departure from Egypt → the miraculous Red Sea crossing.
> 5. **The New Testament fulfilment:** Jesus is called "our Passover Lamb" (1 Corinthians 5:7; John 1:29). Jesus celebrated the Passover meal with His disciples the night before He died. His sacrifice covers our sins just as the lamb's blood protected Israel, bringing us out of slavery to sin into new life.

## The Bible Story

### 1. Israel's Slavery in Egypt

Long before Moses was born, Joseph and his family had moved to Egypt during a great famine. God blessed the Israelites, and over many generations they grew into a large and mighty people. But years later, a new Pharaoh came to power who did not remember Joseph. He looked at the growing Israelite population with fear and suspicion.

Pharaoh forced the Israelites into bitter, cruel slavery. He placed brutal taskmasters over them, forcing them to make bricks and work relentlessly in the fields. The Israelites groaned under their heavy burdens and cried out for help. 

Did God hear them? **Yes!** Scripture tells us: *"God heard their groaning and He remembered His covenant with Abraham, with Isaac, and with Jacob. God saw the sons of Israel and He took notice"* (Exodus 2:24–25). 

God called Moses at the burning bush and sent him back to Egypt to stand before Pharaoh with a clear divine command: **"Let my people go, so that they may worship Me."**

### 2. The Plagues on Egypt

Moses and his brother Aaron went to Pharaoh, but Pharaoh arrogantly answered: *"Who is the LORD, that I should obey His voice and let Israel go? I do not know the LORD, and I will not let Israel go!"* (Exodus 5:2).

Pharaoh made their work even harder. Because Pharaoh stubbornly refused to release the people, God displayed His supreme sovereignty over Egypt and all its false gods through ten powerful plagues:
- Water turned to blood
- Frogs covering the land
- Gnats swarming everywhere
- Swarms of flies
- A severe plague on Egyptian livestock
- Painful boils breaking out on people and animals
- Devastating hail and fire falling from the sky
- Swarms of locusts devouring every plant
- A thick, heavy darkness blanketing Egypt for three days

Throughout these signs and wonders, Pharaoh continually hardened his heart. Even when he pretended to yield, as soon as relief came, he broke his word and refused to let God's people go. Finally, God announced one final, solemn judgment: the death of the firstborn throughout the land of Egypt.

### 3. The Passover Night

God gave Moses very specific instructions for His people so they would be spared from this final judgment. 

Every Israelite household was to take a lamb — a male without defect. On the fourteenth day of the month at twilight, they were to sacrifice the lamb. Then, they were to take some of the blood and apply it to the two doorposts and the lintel above the entrance of their homes.

God commanded them to roast the lamb and eat it that very night with bitter herbs and **unleavened bread**. They were to eat it in haste: with their cloaks tucked into their belts, their sandals on their feet, and their walking staffs in their hands — fully dressed and ready to march at a moment's notice!

Then God declared:
> *"The blood on your houses will distinguish them; when I see the blood, I will pass over you. No plague will fall on you to destroy you when I strike the land of Egypt."* — Exodus 12:13 (BSB)

That midnight, judgment came upon Egypt. Every firstborn in Egypt died, from the firstborn of Pharaoh on the throne to the firstborn of the captive in the dungeon. A great cry rose up throughout all of Egypt, for there was not a house without someone dead.

**Teaching Point:** Why were the Israelites spared? Not because they were stronger, wealthier, or morally superior to the Egyptians. They were spared solely because God had mercy on them and they trusted God's word, sheltering under the blood of the lamb.

### 4. The Exodus from Egypt

In the middle of the night, Pharaoh summoned Moses and Aaron and pleaded: *"Get up! Leave my people, both you and the Israelites! Go, worship the LORD as you have requested!"* The Egyptians urged the people to hurry out of the land, saying, *"We will all die!"*

The Israelites gathered their belongings in great haste. They took their bread dough before any yeast was added, wrapping their kneading bowls into their cloaks upon their shoulders. 

After 430 years in Egypt, hundreds of thousands of Israelites walked out into freedom! God led them by day in a towering pillar of cloud to guide their way, and by night in a pillar of fire to give them light.

### 5. The Red Sea Crossing

Israel had barely begun their journey when Pharaoh had another change of heart. He regretted letting his free workforce leave and cried: *"What have we done? We have let Israel go from serving us!"*

Pharaoh rallied his entire royal army: six hundred of the best chariots, horsemen, and his entire fighting force. They charged after the fleeing Israelites and trapped them against the shores of the Red Sea.

When the Israelites looked up and saw Pharaoh's army closing in behind them and the vast waters of the Red Sea stretching out in front of them, panic erupted. They cried out to Moses: *"Was it because there were no graves in Egypt that you brought us into the wilderness to die?"*

Moses stood firm and proclaimed:
> *"Do not be afraid. Stand firm and you will see the deliverance the LORD will bring you today... The LORD will fight for you; you need only to be still."* — Exodus 14:13–14

Then the LORD spoke to Moses: *"Why do you cry out to Me? Tell the sons of Israel to go forward! Lift up your staff and stretch out your hand over the sea, and divide it, so that the Israelites can go through the sea on dry ground."*

The pillar of cloud moved behind Israel, standing between the army of Egypt and the people of God, casting darkness on the pursuers while giving light to Israel all through the night. Moses stretched out his hand over the sea, and all that night the LORD drove back the sea with a fierce east wind. The waters divided, standing like solid walls on their right hand and on their left!

The Israelites marched right through the middle of the sea on completely dry ground. When the Egyptian army charged in after them with their chariots and horses, the LORD threw the Egyptian army into confusion. God told Moses to stretch his hand out over the sea again. At dawn, the waters rushed back over Pharaoh's chariots and horsemen — not one of the pursuing army survived. 

On that day, the LORD completely delivered Israel out of the hand of Egypt!

### 6. The Lamb of God

The Passover was not merely a historical rescue; it was a prophetic picture pointing forward to God's ultimate plan of salvation.

Centuries later, John the Baptist saw Jesus walking toward him and proclaimed:
> *"Look, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world!"* — John 1:29

When Jesus gave His life on the cross outside Jerusalem, He died at the very time of the Passover festival! The Apostle Paul explicitly connects the two:
> *"For Christ, our Passover Lamb, has been sacrificed."* — 1 Corinthians 5:7

Just as the blood of the unblemished lamb marked the doorposts in Egypt and protected God's people from death, so Jesus' blood cleanses us from all sin and protects everyone who trusts in Him from eternal judgment. And just as Israel did not earn their rescue from slavery, we cannot earn our forgiveness from sin. God provides the Lamb; we receive His rescue through faith.

## The Deeper Meaning: Three Things to Remember

### 1. Rescue Is Entirely God's Work of Grace
The Israelites were helpless slaves. They did not stage an army revolt, build warships, or dig an escape tunnel. God heard their helpless cry and stepped in. The lamb's blood on the doorposts was not an achievement Israel could boast about; it was an act of humble faith and obedience. Salvation has always been 100% God's gift, received by faith.

### 2. When God Rescues, He Makes a Way Where There Is None
At the Red Sea, Israel had mountains on their sides, the sea in front, and an armed army behind. From any human perspective, escape was impossible. But God is never cornered. When there seemed to be no way forward, God made a dry highway straight through the deep waters. We can trust God with situations that feel completely impossible.

### 3. Jesus Is Our Ultimate Passover Lamb
Every lamb sacrificed on Passover was a signpost pointing ahead to Jesus Christ. Jesus is the sinless, perfect Lamb of God. By His blood shed on the cross, we are delivered from slavery to sin and death into eternal life with God.

## Age Adaptations

<AgeGroup level="preschool">
### Younger Children
For younger children, keep the narrative visual, rhythmic, and clear:
- **Focus on the 3 big steps:**
  1. *God's people were sad slaves.*
  2. *God said: 'Put the lamb's blood on the door, and I will pass over you!'*
  3. *God opened the big sea so everyone could walk across safely!*
- **Simple Question:** *"Who rescued the people — did they rescue themselves, or did God rescue them?"*
- **Simple Answer:** God rescued them!
- **Take-Home Phrase:** *"God makes a way!"*
- Use simple motions: spread arms wide like the parting sea; point upward to God.
</AgeGroup>

<AgeGroup level="upper-elementary">
### Older Children
For older elementary students, explore the deeper biblical theology:
- **Examine Exodus 12:1–14 closely:** Notice the requirements of the Passover lamb — male, a year old, without blemish or defect (Exodus 12:5). Compare this directly with 1 Peter 1:18–19 (redeemed with the precious blood of Christ, a lamb without blemish or defect).
- **Grace vs. Works:** Contrast human effort with God's deliverance. Ask: *"What if an Israelite had built a very strong wooden gate but didn't put blood on the doorpost? Would they have been safe?"* (No! Safety was found solely in trusting God's provision.)
- **The Feast of Unleavened Bread:** Clarify how the 7-day feast followed Passover, symbolizing a holy break from Egyptian life and living a sincere, truthful life before God (1 Cor 5:7–8).
- **Challenge:** Ask students to identify "impossible situations" in their lives where they need to trust God to make a way.
</AgeGroup>

## Discussion Questions

### Understanding
1. Why were the Israelites suffering in Egypt? (They were forced into cruel slavery by Pharaoh.)
2. What did God command the Israelites to put on their doorposts on Passover night? (The blood of a lamb without blemish.)
3. Why did the Israelites eat unleavened bread on the night of the exodus? (They were leaving in such a hurry that there was no time to wait for the yeast to make the dough rise.)
4. What happened when Moses stretched his staff over the Red Sea? (The LORD parted the waters with a strong east wind, and Israel crossed on dry ground.)
5. What does the New Testament call Jesus in 1 Corinthians 5:7 and John 1:29? (The Lamb of God; our Passover Lamb.)

### Thinking
6. Why did God judge Pharaoh and the land of Egypt with ten plagues? (Pharaoh repeatedly hardened his heart, refused to obey the true God, and would not let God's people go.)
7. Did the Israelites do anything to "earn" their rescue from slavery? Why is that important for us to know? (No; salvation is God's gift of grace received through faith and obedience.)
8. How does the lamb's blood on the doorposts foreshadow what Jesus did on the cross? (Just as the lamb's blood sheltered Israel from death, Jesus' blood cleanses our sin and saves us from eternal judgment.)
9. Why did God lead Israel through the Red Sea instead of around it? What did that teach Israel about God's power? (It proved beyond doubt that their deliverance was by God's mighty hand alone.)

### Personal Application
10. Is there an "impossible" situation you or your family are facing right now where you need God's help?
11. How does knowing that Jesus is our Passover Lamb give you peace and joy today?
12. When God tells us to follow Him, why is it important to obey quickly rather than delaying?

## Memory Verse

**Exodus 12:13 (BSB)**
> *"The blood on your houses will distinguish them; when I see the blood, I will pass over you. No plague will fall on you to destroy you when I strike the land of Egypt."*

### Preschool Adaptation
For preschool and kindergarten children, shorten the verse to the central phrase:
> **"When I see the blood, I will pass over you."** — Exodus 12:13

### Teaching Method
1. **Visual Demonstration:** Draw a simple doorframe on a whiteboard (the top lintel and two side doorposts). With a red marker, draw red marks on both posts and the lintel.
2. **Interactive Questioning:** 
   - Ask: *"What was on the doorposts?"* → Children call out: **"Blood!"**
   - Ask: *"Who saw the blood?"* → Children call out: **"God!"**
   - Ask: *"What did God do?"* → Children call out: **"Passed over!"**
3. **Call and Response Practice:**
   - Teacher says: *"The blood on your houses will distinguish them;"* → Children echo.
   - Teacher says: *"when I see the blood, I will pass over you."* → Children echo.
   - Teacher says: *"No plague will fall on you to destroy you"* → Children echo.
   - Teacher says: *"when I strike the land of Egypt."* → Children echo.
   - Repeat several times until children can recite the key phrase confidently together.

## Application

Gather the children and ask: **"Have you ever tried to fix a problem by yourself and realized you were completely stuck?"**

Let a few children share. Then explain:
- The Israelites could not break out of Egypt on their own. They were completely powerless slaves.
- But God did not tell them: *"Train harder and build weapons."* 
- God told them: **"Trust My word, shelter under the blood of the lamb, and I will deliver you."**

We have an even bigger problem than Pharaoh's army: our own sin, which separates us from a holy God. We cannot fix our sin by just trying harder, behaving better, or pretending it isn't there. 

Jesus is our Passover Lamb! When we trust in Jesus, His blood covers our sin and gives us forgiveness and eternal life. We don't earn God's rescue — we receive it with thankful hearts!

## Take-Home Challenge

This week, remember that God is the One who makes a way:

### LOOK
Look at a doorway in your home each morning when you leave your bedroom. Remember the blood on the doorposts and whisper: **"Jesus is my Passover Lamb."**

### TRUST
When you face something that feels too big or impossible this week, pause and pray: **"God, You parted the Red Sea. I know You can make a way for me."**

### TELL
Tell someone in your family or a friend why the Israelites ate flat bread: because God rescued them in a hurry! Share that Jesus rescued us completely on the cross.

## Closing Thought

The night of the first Passover was dark and terrifying across Egypt. But inside the homes marked by the blood of the lamb, there was quiet peace and expectant hope. 

God's people were safe — not because their walls were thick, but because God was faithful to His promise.

Later, at the Red Sea, when all hope seemed lost, God parted the ocean deep and led them through on dry ground.

Whatever fears or trials you face, remember this: **God is a rescuer.** He sent His Son, Jesus Christ, to be our Passover Lamb. When you trust in Him, you are safe in His everlasting arms!
