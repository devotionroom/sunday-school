## Teacher Preparation

**Before class:**
- Read Luke 19:1-10 (BSB). Pay attention to the contrast between the grumbling, self-righteous crowd (verse 7) and the radically repentant tax collector (verse 8).
- Prepare the fake coins or tokens for *The Tax Collector Game*.
- Write the memory verse on the board.
- Decide which age adaptation will work best for your classroom.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A note for the teacher**
> It is easy to teach this lesson as simply "a story about a short man who climbed a tree." Don't miss the profound theology here! Zacchaeus was considered a traitor to the Jewish people. Jesus intentionally sought out the most hated man in Jericho to demonstrate that grace is for everyone. Zacchaeus's extreme generosity at the end is the visible proof that salvation had come to his house. 

## Opening Activity: The VIP Treatment

Ask the children:
*"If the President, or a famous athlete, or your favorite singer came to our town today, who do you think they would want to have dinner with?"* (The mayor, other famous people, rich people, good people).
*"Would they pick the meanest bully in your school to have dinner with?"* (No!).

Explain:
*"Usually, important people only want to hang out with other important, good people. But Jesus is the King of the Universe, and when He came to the city of Jericho, He skipped all the 'good' people and chose to have dinner with the biggest bully in town!"*

## The Bible Story

### 1. The Hated Tax Collector
In the city of Jericho, there lived a man named Zacchaeus. He had two main characteristics: he was very short, and he was very rich. 

Zacchaeus was the *chief* tax collector. In those days, the Romans ruled over Israel, and they hired Jewish men to collect taxes from their own neighbors. Tax collectors were famous for stealing. If a person owed 10 coins, Zacchaeus would force them to pay 20 coins, and he would keep the extra for himself. Because of this, everyone in Jericho hated him. They viewed him as a thief, a liar, and a traitor.

### 2. Climbing the Tree
One day, Jesus passed through Jericho. Everyone wanted to see Him! A massive crowd lined the streets. 

Zacchaeus wanted to see Jesus, too. But because he was so short, all the tall people in the crowd blocked his view. No one was going to let the hated tax collector step to the front of the line. So, Zacchaeus did something very undignified for a wealthy, grown man. He ran ahead of the crowd and climbed up into a sycamore-fig tree. 

### 3. Called by Name
Jesus walked down the road, surrounded by hundreds of people pushing and shouting. But when Jesus got to the sycamore tree, He stopped. He didn't just walk past the hated tax collector. Jesus looked right up into the branches.

> *"When Jesus came to that place, He looked up and said, 'Zacchaeus, come down quickly, because I must stay at your house today.'"* (Luke 19:5, BSB)

Jesus knew his name! Out of all the 'good' people in the crowd, Jesus chose to go to the house of the worst sinner in town. Zacchaeus scrambled down the tree and welcomed Jesus gladly.

### 4. The Grumbling Crowd
The people in the crowd were furious. 
> *"All the people saw this and began to mutter, 'He has gone to be the guest of a sinner.'"* (Luke 19:7, BSB)

They thought Jesus had made a mistake. They thought God only loved people who obeyed the rules. They couldn't understand why a holy Teacher would want to eat dinner with a lying thief.

### 5. A Changed Heart
But something amazing happened inside Zacchaeus's house. Being in the presence of Jesus completely changed him. Jesus didn't yell at him or demand that he pay people back. Jesus just loved him. And that love melted Zacchaeus's greedy heart.

Zacchaeus stood up and made a shocking announcement:
> *"Look, Lord! Here and now I give half of my possessions to the poor, and if I have cheated anybody out of anything, I will pay back four times the amount."* (Luke 19:8, BSB)

Zacchaeus didn't just say "I'm sorry." He proved he was sorry by fixing what he broke! If he stole $10 from someone, he was going to give them $40! 

Jesus smiled and said, *"Today salvation has come to this house... For the Son of Man came to seek and to save the lost."* Zacchaeus was no longer a lost, greedy thief. He was a forgiven child of God.

## The Deeper Meaning

Why did Zacchaeus pay back four times what he stole? Under the Old Testament law, if you stole a sheep, you had to pay back four sheep (Exodus 22:1). But Zacchaeus hadn't been caught in a trial; he volunteered to do this! 

This teaches us a huge lesson about repentance. **Repentance** doesn't just mean crying or saying "I'm sorry" so you don't get in trouble. True repentance means your heart actually changes, and you try to make things right. Because Zacchaeus truly loved Jesus, he no longer loved his money. Grace didn't make Zacchaeus lazy; grace made him incredibly generous!

## Three Things to Remember

### 1. Jesus Knows Your Name
Jesus was surrounded by a massive, noisy crowd, but He stopped specifically for Zacchaeus. Jesus sees you, He knows your name, and He knows exactly what is going on in your heart.
*Teacher Question:* How does it make you feel to know that the Creator of the universe knows your name?

### 2. Jesus Loves "Sinners"
The crowd thought Zacchaeus was too bad to be loved by God. But Jesus said He came to seek and save the *lost*. You can never be too bad, too mean, or too messed up for Jesus to love you and save you.
*Teacher Question:* How should we treat kids at school that everyone else thinks are "bad" or "annoying"?

### 3. Grace Changes How We Act
Zacchaeus didn't pay the money back *so* Jesus would love him. He paid the money back *because* Jesus already loved him. When Jesus changes our hearts, it changes the way we treat other people.
*Teacher Question:* If you steal your brother's toy, what is the difference between just saying "sorry" and actually repenting? (Repenting means giving the toy back and choosing not to steal anymore).

## Age Adaptations

<AgeGroup level="preschool">
### Younger Children

For younger children (ages 5–7), focus on Zacchaeus climbing the tree and Jesus wanting to be his friend.

**Simplified concepts:**
- Zacchaeus was a very short man who did bad things. He stole money from people.
- Because he was short, he couldn't see Jesus over the crowd. So he climbed a tall tree!
- Jesus looked up and said, "Zacchaeus, come down! I am coming to your house today!"
- Jesus loved Zacchaeus, and that love made Zacchaeus want to be a good person.
- He gave all the money back to the people!

**Developmentally appropriate questions:**
- Why did Zacchaeus climb the tree? (Because he was short and wanted to see Jesus).
- Was Jesus mean to Zacchaeus? (No, He went to his house for dinner).
- Does Jesus want to be our friend even if we have done bad things? (Yes!).
</AgeGroup>

<AgeGroup level="upper-elementary">
### Older Children

For older children (ages 8–11), focus on the crowd's self-righteousness vs. Zacchaeus's true repentance.

**Deeper discussion:**
Pre-teens have a very strong sense of "fairness." They might actually agree with the crowd—it isn't fair that Jesus hangs out with the bully! Help them see their own need for grace.

**Discussion Scenarios:**
- *Scenario 1:* Imagine the worst bully in your grade. Now imagine the coolest person in the world comes to your school and gives the bully a front-row ticket to a concert. Would you be mad? That's how the crowd felt! Why does Jesus' grace sometimes make us uncomfortable? (Because it reminds us that we don't *earn* His love).
- *Concept:* Restitution (making things right). If you break your mom's vase and say "I'm sorry," she will forgive you. But true repentance means offering to sweep it up or pay for a new one. How did Zacchaeus practice restitution?
</AgeGroup>

## Discussion Questions

### Understanding
1. What was Zacchaeus's job, and why did people hate him?
2. Why did Zacchaeus climb the sycamore tree?
3. What did Jesus say when He looked up into the tree?
4. Why was the crowd grumbling and complaining?
5. How did Zacchaeus prove that his heart had changed?

### Thinking
6. If Jesus knew Zacchaeus was a thief, why do you think He wanted to go to his house?
7. Did Jesus force Zacchaeus to give the money back? (No, Jesus' love inspired him to do it).
8. Who were the "lost" people that Jesus came to seek and save? (All of us!).

### Personal Application
9. Have you ever felt like you did something too bad for God to forgive you? What does this story tell you about that?
10. Is there anything in your life right now that you need to make right? (e.g., apologizing to someone you hurt, or returning something you took).
11. How can you be like Jesus this week to someone who is left out or unpopular?

## Application

When we realize how much Jesus loves us, it makes us want to be generous and kind. If you have done something wrong, don't just hide like the old Zacchaeus. Confess it to Jesus, and ask Him to help you make it right. Remember, Jesus came to save the lost!

## Take-Home Challenge

This week, practice "restitution." If you hurt someone's feelings, don't just say "sorry." Try to say "I'm sorry, and I want to make it right. What can I do to help you?" 

## Closing Thought

The crowd saw a short, greedy thief. But Jesus saw a lost child who needed to be found. Jesus didn't wait for Zacchaeus to clean up his act and become a good person before He loved him. Jesus loved him *first*, and that love is what cleaned Zacchaeus up. No matter what mistakes you have made, Jesus is calling your name, and He wants to change your heart today.

## Closing Prayer

"Heavenly Father, thank You for sending Jesus to seek and to save the lost. Thank You that You know our names and love us, even when we make huge mistakes. Change our hearts just like You changed Zacchaeus. Make us generous, honest, and brave enough to make things right when we do wrong. In Jesus' name, Amen."
